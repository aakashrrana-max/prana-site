03 — Single implant vs full arch

One tooth, or a whole arch?

Implant treatment usually falls into one of two paths. Which one fits depends on how many teeth are affected and the health of the bone and gums, which is what the exam and 3D scan are for.

Single implant is typically for

  • One missing tooth, or one tooth that can't be saved
  • Healthy neighboring teeth you'd rather not file down for a bridge
  • Replacing a tooth without a removable appliance

Full arch is typically for

  • Most or all teeth in the upper or lower jaw missing or failing
  • Current denture wearers who want something fixed in place
  • Replacing many failing teeth in one coordinated plan

04 — Treatment options explained

Five ways to replace teeth

The common options in plain language, with traditional dentures for comparison. The right choice depends on your mouth, your budget, and your goals.

OptionHow it worksProsCons
01

Single implant

From $1,999

A titanium post replaces the tooth root, topped with an abutment and a crown. It stands on its own, without relying on the teeth next to it.

  • Doesn't involve neighboring teeth
  • Fixed; brushed and flossed like a natural tooth
  • Needs healing time before the final crown
  • May need grafting if bone is thin
02

Implant bridge

Priced at consult

Two or more implants support a bridge that replaces several teeth in a row, so you don't need one implant for every missing tooth.

  • Replaces several adjacent teeth with fewer implants
  • Fixed in place
  • Cleaning under the bridge takes extra care
  • Depends on bone where the implants go
03

Implant-supported denture

Snap-on / overdenture · Priced at consult

A denture that clips onto a small number of implants. It's far more stable than a traditional denture but still comes out for cleaning.

  • Much less slipping than a regular denture
  • Often fewer implants than a fixed arch
  • Removable; daily take-out and cleaning
  • Attachments wear and need periodic replacement
04

Fixed full arch

All-on-4 / All-on-X · From $9,999 per arch

A complete arch of teeth attached to several implants (often four to six). Only your dentist removes it. In many cases, provisional teeth are placed on surgery day.

  • Fixed; doesn't come out at home
  • Covers the palate less than an upper denture
  • A surgical procedure with healing time
  • Needs professional cleanings and check-ups
05

Traditional dentures

For comparison · Priced at consult

A removable set of teeth held by suction and fit against the gums. No surgery, and usually the lowest upfront cost of these options.

  • No surgery; shorter process
  • Lower upfront cost
  • Can slip, especially lower dentures
  • Doesn't stimulate the jawbone; may need relines over time

08 — Frequently asked questions

Questions we hear most

Does getting implants hurt?

The area is numbed for the procedure, and many patients describe it as more pressure than pain. Soreness and swelling for a few days afterward are common. Sedation options can be discussed at your consult; whether sedation is right for you depends on your health history.

How long do implants last?

Implants are designed as a long-term solution, and many last for many years. How long they last depends on things like home care, regular check-ups, overall health, and habits such as smoking or grinding.

Am I too old for implants?

Age alone usually isn't the deciding factor. What matters more is your overall health, bone, and gums, which is why we start with an exam and a 3D scan.

I've been told I have bone loss. Can I still get implants?

Often, yes, but it depends on how much bone is there and where. Options can include bone grafting, a sinus lift, or angled implants used in some full-arch plans. The 3D scan shows what's possible.

What if I have diabetes or I smoke?

Both can affect healing, so an exam and a review of your health history are needed before anyone can say whether implants are a good fit. Well-managed conditions don't automatically rule implants out.

How many visits will it take?

A single implant usually involves a consult and scan, placement, and a final crown visit, plus healing check-ins. Full arch adds a planning step. Your plan will list your visits.

Can I get teeth the same day?

With full-arch treatment, fixed provisional (temporary) teeth can often be placed on surgery day when clinically appropriate. The final arch follows after healing.

Why do implant prices vary so much in Miami?

Quotes often include different things: number of implants, prosthesis material (acrylic vs zirconia), extractions, grafting, sedation. Ask for an itemized written quote to compare like with like.

What happens at the free consultation?

An exam, a 3D (CBCT) scan, a conversation about your goals and options, and an itemized written quote. There's no obligation to go ahead.
