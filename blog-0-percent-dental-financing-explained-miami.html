0% financing for dental work: how it actually works

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: 0% financing lets you spread out the cost of dental work over monthly payments with no added interest, subject to credit approval. At Prana, plans start from $167/month, and checking your rate doesn't affect your credit score.

Cost is one of the biggest reasons people put off dental work they know they need, sometimes for years, until a small problem becomes a bigger and more expensive one. 0% financing exists specifically to remove that barrier by spreading a larger cost into smaller monthly payments without adding interest on top of the total. Here's how it actually works, in plain terms, including what "0%" really means in practice and what actually happens if you go ahead and check your rate.

Why dental financing exists in the first place

Many dental procedures, especially implants or an All-on-4 full arch, are meaningful expenses that most people don't budget for as a single lump sum. Financing exists to let patients get treatment when they need it rather than delaying necessary or wanted care until they've saved the full amount upfront, which for many people could mean waiting years. It's a common option across dental practices, not something unique to any one provider — but the specific terms, approval process, and monthly cost vary a lot from one place to another, so it's genuinely worth understanding all of the details rather than simply assuming all financing offers are the same across every dental office you might visit. A financing offer that sounds similar on the surface can end up costing very differently once the fine print is actually read carefully from start to finish, front to back, before signing anything.

How 0% APR financing works generally

With 0% financing, you're approved to pay off the total cost over a set number of months, and — unlike a typical credit card or loan — no interest is added on top of that total as long as you're within the plan's terms. You pay the same amount you would have paid upfront, just spread into monthly installments instead of one large payment all at once. Approval is based on creditworthiness, similar to any other financing product, and terms like the monthly payment amount and plan length depend on the total cost of treatment and the plan you qualify for based on your credit. It's worth reading the plan terms carefully to confirm the interest rate genuinely stays at zero for the full term, rather than reverting to a standard rate later.

What Prana's financing plan looks like

0% financing is available at Prana starting from $167 a month, on approved credit. The exact monthly payment depends on the total treatment cost and the specific plan terms you qualify for, which are reviewed during the financing conversation as part of your visit, so you know the real number before agreeing to anything, rather than working backward from a rough guess made before your exam. It can be applied toward treatments like implants, full-arch restorations, Invisalign, veneers, and other larger treatment plans where spreading out the cost makes the decision considerably easier.

How the application process typically works

Financing is generally discussed once you have a treatment plan and cost estimate in hand, so the monthly payment you're looking at reflects your actual treatment rather than a rough guess pulled from an average. From there, checking your rate is a short, low-friction step, and if you decide to move forward, the plan terms are laid out clearly before you commit to anything, so there are no surprises about the total once payments are complete and the treatment is finished.

Checking your rate won't hurt your credit

One common hesitation is worrying that even checking financing options will show up as a hard inquiry and ding your credit score, which keeps a lot of people from even looking into it at all, even when financing would genuinely help them get needed treatment sooner rather than later. At Prana, checking your rate does not affect your credit — it's a preliminary check, not a full application, so there's genuinely no downside at all to seeing what you'd qualify for before deciding whether to move forward with treatment.

Combining financing with insurance

Financing and dental insurance aren't mutually exclusive — if you have insurance, it can cover part of the cost, and financing can cover the remaining portion you're responsible for out of pocket. Most major insurance is accepted, and your exact coverage is confirmed in writing at your free consult, so you'll know your real out-of-pocket number before deciding how much, if any, of it to finance rather than guessing at what insurance will actually cover.
