how 3d scanning replaced messy dental impressions

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Digital 3D scanning has largely replaced the tray-and-putty impressions that used to gag patients and warp with handling. A handheld scanner captures your teeth in minutes, giving a more accurate, more comfortable starting point for crowns, veneers, Invisalign, and implants.

If you've ever bitten down on a tray of thick, cold putty and had to hold still while it set, you know why patients dread dental impressions. That method is disappearing from most modern dental offices. At Prana, impressions are now taken with a small handheld digital scanner instead of a tray — here's what changed, why it happened, and why it actually matters for the accuracy of your treatment, not just your comfort.

What the old method involved

Traditional impressions use a tray filled with a putty-like material that's pressed over your teeth and held in place for a minute or two while it sets. It's uncomfortable for most people and can trigger a gag reflex for many, especially when the tray covers the back teeth or the roof of the mouth. Beyond comfort, the material itself is a risk factor for accuracy — it can shrink slightly as it sets, tear on removal, or trap small air bubbles, any of which distorts the final mold. A distorted mold usually isn't obvious until the lab calls back to say the impression wasn't usable, or until a crown or aligner comes back and doesn't fit quite right the first time, meaning another appointment just to redo the whole step from scratch.

How digital scanning works instead

A digital scanner is a small wand that captures thousands of images of your teeth per second as it passes over them, stitching those images together into a precise 3D model on a screen in real time. There's no tray, no putty, and no waiting for material to set — the dentist simply guides the wand around your mouth in a series of smooth passes. Because you can see the model build on screen as it happens, gaps or unclear areas are obvious immediately and can be rescanned on the spot, rather than discovered days later when a lab calls to say the mold wasn't usable and the whole appointment has to be repeated. The whole scan usually takes just a few minutes from start to finish.

Why it's more comfortable

Without a tray filling your mouth, there's far less triggering of the gag reflex, and the scanning wand itself is open and easy to breathe around throughout the process. Most patients find the process quick and far less unpleasant than a traditional impression, and there's no unpleasant taste or texture to deal with while material sets in your mouth. This matters most for anxious patients, people with a strong gag reflex, and kids — comfort during routine steps like this is part of what keeps people from avoiding dental visits altogether, which matters more for your long-term oral health than the scan itself. A less stressful visit today makes the next one easier to schedule too.

Where the accuracy gain shows up

A digital scan doesn't shrink, warp, or tear the way physical impression material can, and it captures fine detail — like the exact contour where a crown margin needs to sit — with more consistency from one scan to the next. That precision matters most for restorations that need a tight fit: crowns, veneers, and the surgical planning for dental implants, where a small measurement error can mean the difference between a comfortable fit and one that needs adjusting. It's also the same technology behind clear aligner treatment, since Invisalign trays are manufactured directly from a digital scan of your bite rather than a physical mold that could shift slightly before it's poured into a model.

How this speeds up the rest of your treatment

Because a digital file can be sent instantly to a design program or a milling machine, it removes an entire step that used to slow treatment down — there's no impression to package and ship, and no risk of it getting damaged in transit. That's part of what makes it possible for restorations designed and milled through Prana's in-house lab to move from scan to finished piece quickly, sometimes within the same visit. The scan itself also becomes a useful record your dentist can reference at future visits to track changes over time, without needing to take a fresh impression each time something needs comparing.

What to expect at your visit

Every free first-visit consult at Prana includes a 3D scan as part of the exam, along with X-rays, at no cost. If you've avoided the dentist in the past because of impressions specifically, it's worth mentioning that when you book your visit — most patients are surprised how quick and easy the scan is compared to what they remember from a past tray-and-putty impression, and it's a good reason to give a routine checkup another try.
