Who's a good candidate for All-on-4

All-on-4 is generally considered for patients who are missing most or all of the teeth in an arch, or who have teeth that are failing beyond repair and want a fixed, non-removable solution rather than a traditional denture. It typically requires enough bone to support four implants, along with overall health that allows for oral surgery, both of which Dr. Rana evaluates with a 3D scan rather than guessing from a photo or a phone call. Age alone isn't a barrier. What matters is bone volume and overall oral health, both of which can often be improved beforehand if needed. It isn't the right fit for everyone, and a more conservative recommendation, including single implants or other restorative options, is sometimes the more appropriate answer.

Two people can both be told they need a full arch and walk away with very different quotes. That is usually not a pricing trick. It reflects real differences between mouths: how many teeth are still there, how healthy the bone is, and what the final teeth are made of. How to compare All-on-4 quotes line by line

Lay every quote you collect side by side and check that each one answers the same list. If a line is missing, ask about it before you compare the bottom numbers.

  • Implants: how many per arch, and whether that count could change after the CT scan.
  • Abutments: the connectors that join the implants to the teeth. They belong in the quote, not on a later bill.
  • Extractions: how many teeth, and whether difficult ones are priced differently.
  • Grafting: whether it is expected, possible or ruled out, and what it would cost if it turns out to be needed.
  • Sedation: which type, and whether it is part of the price.
  • Provisional arch: included or separate, and roughly how long you will wear it.
  • Final arch: the material (acrylic or zirconia) and when it will be made.
  • Healing visits: how many follow-ups and adjustments come with the quote.

Also confirm whether a number is per arch or for both jaws. A quote for upper and lower together can look alarming next to a single-arch quote from another office, even when the per-arch price is close.

Does insurance help pay for a full arch?

Sometimes, though usually only a little. Many dental plans have annual maximums that are often in the $1,000 to $2,000 range, so even a plan that includes implants may cover only part of a full arch. Some plans have waiting periods or exclude implants entirely, and Original Medicare generally doesn't cover dental implants, though some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits. Questions, answered

Is All-on-4 cheaper than getting an implant for every tooth?

Usually, yes. Replacing a whole arch one tooth at a time would take many separate implants and crowns, while All-on-4 holds a full row of teeth on four implants. For comparison, a single implant with abutment and crown at Prana starts from $1,999 and a full arch starts from $9,999. Which approach fits you depends on your bone and how many healthy teeth you still have.

How much does All-on-4 cost for top and bottom?

Each arch is priced on its own. At Prana, All-on-4 starts from $9,999 per arch, so treating both jaws means two arch prices, each adjusted for what that jaw needs. The upper and lower can differ, for example when one needs grafting or extractions and the other does not. Your written quote shows each arch separately.

Does dental insurance pay for All-on-4?

It may pay for part of it. Many plans have yearly maximums often in the $1,000 to $2,000 range, and some exclude implants or have waiting periods, so coverage for a full arch is usually partial at most. Original Medicare generally does not cover implants. Bring your card and we check your benefits for you.

Is zirconia worth the extra cost for All-on-4?

It depends on your bite, your habits and your budget. Zirconia resists wear and staining, which some people value over the long run, while acrylic costs less and is simpler to repair. People who grind their teeth may need a night guard with either material. Dr. Rana can walk you through the tradeoffs for your case before you choose.
