Child knocked out a baby tooth?
Leave it out, and here is what to do instead.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

If your child knocked out a baby tooth, don't put it back in the socket. Unlike an adult tooth, a baby tooth that is pushed back can damage the permanent tooth forming just above or below it. Stop the bleeding, check your child for signs of a head injury, keep the tooth to show the dentist, and call for a same-day visit. This guide also covers chipped, pushed-in and loose baby teeth, and how to tell a baby tooth from an adult one.

Short answer: a knocked-out baby tooth should stay out. Press clean gauze on the gum for about 10 minutes, use something cold on the lip, and look for head injury signs such as vomiting, confusion or unusual sleepiness, which mean the ER. Then call a dentist the same day so the area and the adult tooth underneath can be checked.

The first few minutes

Mouth injuries bleed a lot, and a small cut mixed with saliva can look far worse than it is. Your calm voice helps more than anything else right now. Then work through these steps:

  • Look at your child as a whole first: are they alert, acting like themselves, breathing easily? Check for head injury signs (see below) before you focus on the tooth.
  • Have them rinse with cool water, or wipe the mouth gently with a clean, damp cloth, so you can see where the bleeding comes from.
  • Press a folded piece of clean gauze or cloth on the bleeding spot and hold it steadily for about 10 minutes without peeking.
  • Hold something cold against the outside of the lip, wrapped in a thin cloth. Older kids may prefer to suck on an ice pop.
  • Find the tooth if you can and keep it in a clean container. You won't put it back, but the dentist will want to see that it came out whole.
  • Give a children's pain reliever only as the label directs for your child's age and weight, or as your pediatrician advises.

Why a knocked-out baby tooth stays out

The adult tooth develops in the jaw right next to the root of the baby tooth. Pushing a baby tooth back into its socket can drive it into that developing tooth, and a replanted baby tooth can also become infected. The small benefit of keeping a baby tooth that would fall out in a few years isn't worth that risk, so dentists generally don't replant them.

Losing a front baby tooth early usually causes fewer problems than parents fear. The dentist will look at whether the space needs watching, especially if a back baby tooth is lost, because neighboring teeth can drift into the gap.

If you can't find the tooth, don't assume it fell out. It may have been pushed up into the gum, which can look exactly like a missing tooth, and only an X-ray can show it. If your child is coughing, wheezing or breathing noisily after the injury, they may have inhaled the tooth or a piece of it: go to the ER.

Baby tooth or adult tooth?

The right first aid depends on which kind of tooth it is, so this matters. A few clues help:

  • Age. The first adult front teeth usually come in somewhere around age six to eight. In a younger child, a knocked-out front tooth is almost always a baby tooth.
  • History. If your child has never lost a tooth in that spot, the tooth is a baby tooth.
  • Look. New adult front teeth are noticeably larger and often have small bumps along the biting edge. Baby teeth are smaller and whiter.
  • Root. A knocked-out baby tooth that was nearly ready to fall out may have very little root left.

If you aren't sure, or the child is older than about six, treat it as an adult tooth and follow our guide to a knocked-out adult tooth, where minutes matter and putting it back is usually the right move.

Chipped, pushed in, loose or moved

Chipped

A small chip with a smooth edge and no pain can usually wait a day or two for a visit. A larger break, or a pink or red dot in the middle of the broken surface, means the nerve may be exposed, so call for a same-day appointment.

Pushed into the gum

A baby tooth driven upward looks shorter than its neighbors, or seems to have disappeared. Many of these slowly come back down on their own over the following weeks or months, but the dentist needs an X-ray to see where the tooth is in relation to the adult tooth.

Loose or pushed out of position

A slightly loose tooth often firms up with a soft diet for a week or two. A tooth bent forward or backward so that your child can't close their teeth normally needs to be seen the same day. A baby tooth that is extremely loose may be removed so it can't be swallowed or inhaled.

A tooth that turns gray later

Days or weeks after a bump, a baby tooth may darken. Sometimes the color fades again; sometimes it means the nerve has died. Let the dentist know, and watch for a pimple on the gum above that tooth, which is a sign of infection.

When to go to the ER instead

Kids often knock out teeth in falls, and a fall hard enough to break a tooth can also injure the head. Go to the ER, or call 911, if your child:

  • Lost consciousness, even briefly
  • Vomits, seems confused, is unusually sleepy or hard to wake, or has a headache that gets worse
  • Has bleeding that doesn't slow after 10 to 15 minutes of firm pressure
  • Has a cut that goes all the way through the lip, or a gaping cut on the face
  • Can't open or close the mouth normally, which can point to a jaw injury
  • Has any trouble breathing, or swelling that spreads across the face

What the dentist checks, now and later

At the visit we look at the gum, lips and tongue, check the bite and test how loose nearby teeth are. A small, gentle X-ray shows the root, any broken piece left in the gum and the adult tooth waiting underneath. Many baby tooth injuries need nothing more than watching, with a follow-up visit or two over the next months.

Because a hit to a baby tooth can occasionally affect the adult tooth forming beside it, a spot or color change may only show up years later when that adult tooth comes in. Mention the old injury at future checkups.

Baby tooth injuries: common questions

Should I put my child's baby tooth back in?

No. A baby tooth pushed back into the socket can harm the adult tooth developing next to its root and can become infected. Keep the tooth in a clean container, control the bleeding and call a dentist the same day.

Is it bad if a child loses a baby tooth too early from an injury?

Often it causes no lasting problem, especially for a front tooth. The dentist checks whether the space needs watching, since nearby teeth can sometimes drift into the gap. A check also confirms the adult tooth underneath wasn't affected.

My child's baby tooth was pushed up into the gum. Will it come back down?

Many pushed-in baby teeth gradually come back down over weeks or months. The dentist takes an X-ray to see the tooth's position and decide whether to wait or remove it. Keep the area clean and watch for swelling or a gum pimple.

Why did my child's tooth turn gray after a fall?

A gray tooth after an injury usually means bleeding inside the tooth or a nerve that has been damaged. The color sometimes improves over time. Tell the dentist, and call sooner if you see swelling or a pimple on the gum.

When should a child go to the ER after a mouth injury?

Go if your child lost consciousness, vomits, seems confused or very sleepy, has bleeding that won't stop with pressure, a cut through the lip, trouble breathing or can't close the mouth normally. A dentist can check the teeth afterward.
