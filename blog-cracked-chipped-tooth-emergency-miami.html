cracked or chipped tooth: is it an emergency?

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: A small chip with no pain and no sharp edges can usually wait for a regular appointment, while a crack with sharp pain, sensitivity to temperature, or a visible piece missing near the nerve needs same-day emergency care. When in doubt, call rather than guess, since some cracks aren't visible without a proper exam.

Not every chipped or cracked tooth means dropping everything right now to find same-day care immediately, but some situations absolutely do call for exactly that kind of urgency. Here's a practical, honest way to sort out which situation you're actually dealing with right now, without relying on guesswork alone, and what happens once you're actually seen by someone who can look closely at the tooth.

Signs it can likely wait for a regular appointment

A small cosmetic chip on the edge of a front tooth, with no pain present, no sharp or jagged edge cutting into your tongue or cheek, and no sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures, usually isn't a true emergency situation at all. It's still genuinely worth getting it evaluated and often repaired with bonding or a veneer for appearance's sake, just not on an urgent, same-day basis that disrupts your whole day and plans. A photo of the chip taken right after it happens can also be genuinely helpful for your dentist to reference later, especially if the edge changes or smooths out naturally before your appointment.

Signs that mean call an emergency dentist today

Sharp, ongoing pain, sensitivity that lingers well after eating or drinking something hot or cold, a visible crack line running down toward the gumline, or a piece of the tooth missing large enough to expose the inner layers underneath it are all genuinely good, valid reasons to go ahead and call an emergency line the same day rather than simply waiting it out at home. These particular signs can point to a crack reaching all the way to the nerve, which genuinely only gets worse the longer it sits untreated without attention or care from a dentist, sometimes progressing from mild sensitivity to constant pain within just a few days once bacteria find their way into the exposed layer.

What to do right away, regardless of severity

Rinse your mouth gently with warm water, save any broken piece of the tooth if you're able to actually find it nearby, and apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek if there's any visible swelling present at all. Avoid chewing on that particular side of your mouth entirely, and cover a sharp edge with dental wax or sugar-free gum if it's cutting into your tongue or cheek until you're actually seen by someone who can help. Over-the-counter pain relief can also help take the edge off in the meantime if needed, and keeping the area clean with gentle rinsing reduces the chance of irritation or infection while you wait to be seen.

Why some cracks aren't visible without an exam

A crack can run below the gumline or straight through the tooth in a direction you simply can't see or feel with your tongue no matter how carefully you check, which is exactly why pain or sensitivity without any obviously visible chip still genuinely deserves a full exam. X-rays combined with a close clinical look are genuinely the only reliable way to map out how far a crack actually extends beneath the visible surface of the tooth. Some cracks even show up more clearly under a special light Dr. Rana uses during the exam itself, or by having you bite down on a small stick to see exactly where the pain actually originates from within the tooth.

What treatment usually looks like

Depending on the severity involved, treatment ranges anywhere from simply smoothing and bonding a minor chip, to placing a full crown for a tooth that's lost significant structure, to a root canal if the crack has genuinely reached all the way to the nerve inside. Your exam determines which category actually applies to you, and Prana's in-house lab means crowns don't require weeks of waiting on an outside lab elsewhere to finish the work needed before you're back to normal.

When to just call instead of deciding yourself

If you're genuinely unsure which category your particular situation falls into, call rather than try to diagnose it entirely yourself from a mirror at home — a quick conversation with the emergency line can often tell you right away whether it's safe to wait until tomorrow or genuinely worth coming in today instead of putting it off any longer than necessary. The free emergency exam and X-ray make that first step easy, whatever the answer ultimately turns out to be, so there's genuinely no good reason to sit with the uncertainty any longer than you actually have to overnight.
