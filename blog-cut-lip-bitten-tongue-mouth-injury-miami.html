Bitten tongue or cut lip?
When it heals on its own, and when it needs stitches.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

A bitten tongue or cut lip usually looks worse than it is: the mouth has a rich blood supply, so even small cuts bleed heavily, and most heal quickly without stitches. A few injuries do need to be closed, though, and a fall hard enough to split a lip can also crack or loosen a tooth. This guide covers how to stop the bleeding, which cuts need stitches at an ER or urgent care, how to check the teeth afterward and what normal healing looks like.

Short answer: press clean gauze firmly on the cut for 10 to 15 minutes without lifting it. Small tongue and cheek bites usually heal on their own in a week or two. Get stitches at an ER or urgent care for a cut that gapes open, goes all the way through the lip, crosses the edge of the lip, or keeps bleeding after steady pressure. Then have a dentist check the teeth.

Stop the bleeding first

Blood mixes with saliva and spreads, so the amount can look alarming. Work through these steps calmly:

  • Wash your hands, then rinse the mouth gently with cool water so you can find where the bleeding is coming from.
  • Fold a piece of clean gauze or a clean cloth and press it firmly on the cut. For a lip, pinch the lip between gauze on the inside and outside. For the tongue, hold the gauze on the cut with your fingers.
  • Keep steady pressure for 10 to 15 minutes. Don't lift it to check; each peek restarts the clock.
  • Sit upright and lean slightly forward so blood doesn't run down the throat, which can cause nausea.
  • Once bleeding slows, hold something cold on the outside of the lip or let an ice chip melt on the tongue to limit swelling.
  • If the bleeding is still steady after 15 minutes of firm pressure, go to the ER.

Does it need stitches?

Most mouth wounds close well on their own because the tissue heals fast. These guidelines reflect what usually happens, but if you are unsure, have it looked at the same day. Stitches tend to work well when placed within hours of the injury, so don't wait until tomorrow to decide.

Tongue, lips, cheeks and gums are different

The tongue

Tongue bites bleed a lot and swell quickly, but they also tend to heal fast. A small cut on the side or tip usually closes within days. Deep cuts that split the tongue's edge are the ones that may need closing.

The lips

Lips swell dramatically after a hit, often more than the cut deserves. The swelling peaks in the first day or two. What matters most is whether the cut goes through or crosses the lip's edge, because a scar there is easy to see.

Cheeks after numbing

A common cheek or lip bite happens after a dental visit, when a child chews on a numb lip without feeling it. Keep kids from eating until the numbness wears off, and watch that they don't nibble the area.

The gums

A gum cut from a fall, a fork or a hard chip usually heals on its own. A gum tear near a tooth, or a tooth that has shifted, needs a dentist.

After a fall, check the teeth too

When a lip is split, attention goes to the blood, and an injured tooth gets missed. Once things calm down, look and feel for:

  • Chips or sharp edges, and any piece of tooth missing
  • A tooth that moves when pressed gently, or sits higher, lower or more forward than before
  • A bite that feels different when you close
  • A small pink or red dot in a broken tooth, which can mean the nerve is exposed
  • Pain with cold or when biting in the days after the fall
  • A tooth that turns darker over the following weeks

Healing, and signs of infection

Within a day or two, a mouth wound usually turns white or yellowish. That is the moist version of a scab, not pus, and it fades as new tissue grows underneath. Soreness is usually worst in the first two or three days, and most small cuts heal within one to two weeks.

To help it along:

  • Rinse gently with warm salt water after meals.
  • Choose soft, cool foods, and skip lime, hot sauce, chips and anything crunchy or salty that stings.
  • Take an over-the-counter pain reliever only as the label directs, if it is safe for you.
  • Don't pick at the area or keep poking it with your tongue.
  • If the cut came from a fall onto the ground or a dirty object, ask your doctor whether your tetanus shot is up to date.

Call a dentist or doctor if pain and swelling get worse after the third day, you see pus or the area smells bad, or you develop a fever. A sore in the mouth that hasn't healed in two weeks should also be checked, even if it doesn't hurt.

Mouth cuts and bites: common questions

When does a bitten tongue need stitches?

Most bitten tongues heal without stitches. A cut that gapes open when the tongue moves, a flap of tissue, or bleeding that continues after 15 minutes of firm pressure usually needs to be seen at an ER or urgent care, where stitches can be placed if needed.

How long does a cut inside the lip take to heal?

Small cuts inside the lip usually heal within one to two weeks, and the soreness eases after the first few days. The white or yellow layer that forms is normal healing tissue. Increasing pain, pus or fever are signs to have it checked.

Should I go to urgent care or the ER for a cut lip?

A cut that goes through the lip or crosses its edge needs stitches, which many urgent care centers can place. Go to the ER instead for heavy bleeding that won't stop, a possible broken jaw, a head injury or trouble breathing or swallowing.

Why is the cut in my mouth white?

A white or yellowish layer over a mouth wound is the normal healing layer, the mouth's version of a scab. It usually appears within a day or two and fades as the area heals. Pus, a bad smell or worsening pain point to infection instead.

Should I see a dentist after splitting my lip in a fall?

Yes, if a tooth feels loose, looks chipped or out of place, or your bite feels different. A tooth can be injured even when it looks fine at first, so a check within a few days is sensible, and the same day if a tooth has moved or broken.
