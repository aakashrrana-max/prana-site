Dental implants vs dentures cost in Miami,
what you pay now and later.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

Comparing dental implants vs dentures cost in Miami starts with one plain fact: dentures almost always cost less on day one, and implants cost more. The fuller picture includes what each option asks of you over the years, from relines and adhesive to cleanings and repairs. This guide covers one missing tooth and a full arch, with the prices we can confirm.

Replacing one tooth: implant, bridge or partial denture

There are three common ways to fill the space left by one tooth, each with its own price pattern.

Single dental implant

A small post goes into the jawbone where the root used to be, and an abutment and crown go on top. The teeth on either side are left alone. At Prana, a single implant starts from $1,999 for the implant, abutment and crown together. Our dental implants page covers the process step by step.

Bridge

A bridge fills the gap with a false tooth held in place by crowns on the neighboring teeth. It is fixed and needs no surgery, but those neighbors have to be reshaped for crowns, even when they were perfectly healthy. Its price depends on the teeth involved, so it is quoted after an exam.

Partial denture

A partial is a removable plate carrying one or more false teeth, held by clasps on your remaining teeth. It is often the lowest upfront cost of the three. It comes out for cleaning and at night, and over time the clasps can put extra stress on the teeth they grip. Like a bridge, it is priced after an exam.

Replacing a full arch: All-on-4 or All-on-X vs complete dentures

For a mouth with no teeth left, or with teeth that can't be saved, the choice usually comes down to a complete denture or a fixed arch on implants.

A complete denture is a full set of teeth on a base that rests on your gums. The upper one covers the roof of your mouth and is held mostly by suction; the lower one has less to hold onto and often moves more. It is the lower-cost route at the start.

An implant-supported arch is a fixed row of teeth attached to implants in the jaw. With All-on-4, four implants carry the arch, and All-on-X uses more when the bone or the bite calls for it. You don't take it out at night, and an upper arch usually leaves the palate uncovered. At Prana, both start from $9,999 per arch.

Upfront vs lifetime cost: what keeps showing up later

The price of a denture or an implant arch is only the first payment.

Over the years with dentures

  • Relines. As the gums and bone underneath change, the fit loosens. A reline adds fresh material to the inside of the denture so it sits snugly again, and most wearers need it more than once.
  • Adhesive. Many people buy denture cream or strips on a steady basis to stop the plate from shifting. It is a small but ongoing expense.
  • Repairs and replacement. Teeth can chip and bases can crack if dropped. After enough years, the mouth changes so much that a new denture makes more sense than another reline.

Over the years with implants

  • Checkups and cleanings. Implants can't get cavities, but the gum and bone around them can become inflamed or infected if plaque builds up, so professional cleanings matter.
  • Wear on the teeth. On a full arch, acrylic teeth wear down slowly and may one day be replaced on the same implants. A single crown can chip or wear as well.
  • A night guard. If you grind your teeth, a guard can help protect the new ones.
  • Home care tools. A water flosser or special floss helps clean under a fixed arch or around an implant.

We aren't putting dollar figures on these, since they vary by office and by mouth. The pattern is what matters: dentures tend to bring a steady trickle of smaller costs, while implants bring a larger cost at the start and, for many people, more modest upkeep after that. How long implants last depends on your health and your home care, which our article on how long dental implants last covers in detail.

Bone loss: the cost you don't see right away

Once a tooth is gone, the bone that held its root no longer gets the same push from chewing, and over time that part of the jaw tends to shrink. Dentures sit on top of the gums and don't stop that process, which is one reason they loosen and need relines.

Implants sit inside the bone and pass chewing forces into it, much like a natural root. For many people, that helps preserve the bone around them. This matters for cost, too: a jaw that has shrunk a lot may need grafting before implants can go in, which raises the price later.

Comfort and daily life

  • Dentures can rub and leave sore spots, especially in the first weeks and whenever the fit starts to drift.
  • An upper denture covers the palate, and some wearers say that dulls the taste and temperature of food.
  • Lower dentures in particular can lift when you talk or laugh.
  • Implant teeth are fixed, so there is nothing to slip, but getting them means surgery and usually a few months of healing before the final teeth.
  • Some people can't have implants for health reasons, or simply don't want surgery. For them, a well-made denture is a sensible choice.

Questions, answered

Are dental implants cheaper than dentures in the long run?

They can be for some people, though not always. Dentures cost less up front but often need relines, adhesive, repairs and eventual replacement. Implants cost more at the start and still need checkups and the occasional repair. Over many years the gap often narrows, but your health and home care make a big difference.

How much do dental implants cost compared to dentures in Miami?

At Prana, a single implant with abutment and crown starts from $1,999 and a full arch on All-on-4 or All-on-X starts from $9,999 per arch. Dentures generally cost less up front, and their price depends on the type and the office. The exact price for your case is confirmed in writing before any treatment.

Can I switch from dentures to implants later?

Often, yes. Plenty of people wear dentures for years and then move to implants. The main question is how much bone remains, since the jaw tends to shrink under a denture. A 3D scan shows whether implants can go in directly or whether grafting would be needed first.

Does insurance cover implants or dentures better?

It depends on the plan. Some plans cover dentures but limit or exclude implants, and many have annual maximums that pay for only part of either. Waiting periods may also apply. Bring your insurance card and we check your benefits for you.

Are implants or dentures better for older adults?

Age alone usually doesn't rule out implants. Your bone, your overall health and any medicines that affect healing matter more. For some older adults dentures are the more practical choice, and for others a fixed arch makes eating and daily life easier.
