Do you need a crown after a root canal?
Usually for back teeth. Here is why.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

Whether you need a crown after a root canal depends mostly on which tooth it is and how much healthy structure is left. Molars and premolars usually need one, because they take heavy chewing force and a treated tooth is more likely to crack. Front teeth sometimes do well with a filling alone. This guide explains how a dentist decides, what happens if you wait, and how to plan the cost.

Short answer: most back teeth need a crown after a root canal to protect them from cracking, and many dentists recommend placing it within a few weeks. A front tooth that lost very little structure may only need a filling. The right answer comes from an exam of your specific tooth.

Why back teeth usually need a crown

A root canal removes infected or inflamed pulp from inside the tooth, then cleans, shapes and seals the canals. It saves the tooth, but it does not make the tooth stronger. To reach the canals, the dentist makes an opening through the chewing surface, and by the time most people need a root canal, decay or an old filling has already taken out a good part of the tooth.

What is left is often a shell: thin walls standing around a filled center. Molars and premolars grind food with a lot of force every day, and a thin wall can split under that load. A crack that runs down into the root often cannot be repaired, and the tooth that was just saved may have to come out.

A crown covers the whole visible part of the tooth, including the cusps (the pointed parts that take most of the force). It holds the walls together, a bit like a cap on a bottle. That is why crowns are the usual recommendation after a root canal on a back tooth.

When a filling alone may be enough

Not every treated tooth needs full coverage. Front teeth (incisors and canines) mostly bite and tear rather than grind, so they carry less force. If the opening for the root canal was small and the tooth had little decay or old filling material, a well-sealed tooth-colored filling can be a reasonable option.

Dentists usually weigh a few things before recommending a filling instead of a crown:

  • How many walls of natural tooth are still standing, and how thick they are
  • Whether the tooth already has cracks or a large old filling
  • Your bite, including signs of clenching or grinding
  • Whether the tooth anchors a bridge or a partial denture, which adds stress
  • How the tooth looks: a darkened front tooth may need a crown or veneer for appearance

If a filling is chosen, the tooth is usually watched at regular checkups so any early crack can be caught. You can read more about how fillings and crowns differ in our guide to signs you need a filling or a crown.

How long can you wait for the crown?

After a root canal, the tooth is often closed with a temporary filling. That temporary seal is meant to last a short time, not months. Saliva and bacteria can slowly leak past it and recontaminate the canals, and the weakened tooth is at risk of breaking each time you chew on it.

Many dentists recommend getting the permanent crown within a few weeks, and your dentist will give you a timeline for your case. Until then, a few simple habits help protect the tooth:

  • Chew on the other side when you can
  • Avoid ice, hard candy, nuts and very sticky foods on that side
  • Keep brushing and flossing normally around the temporary
  • Call if the temporary falls out, the tooth feels loose or a piece breaks off

If the temporary does come out, our post on what to do after you lose a crown or filling covers the first steps until you can be seen.

What the crown visit involves

Before a crown is made, the dentist checks that the root canal is healing and that the tooth has enough structure to hold a crown. If a lot of the tooth is missing, a core buildup (a filling material that rebuilds the inner shape) is placed first. Occasionally a post is placed into a canal to help hold that buildup, though many teeth do not need one.

The tooth is then shaped so the crown can fit over it. At Prana Dental we take a digital 3D scan instead of a tray of putty, and the crown is designed and made with our in-house lab. You wear a temporary crown until the final one is ready, then it is checked for fit, bite and color and cemented in place. Our dental crowns page walks through materials and each step in more detail.

Because the nerve inside a root-canal-treated tooth has been removed, the tooth itself usually feels little during crown work. The gums around it can still be sensitive, so local anesthetic is normally used for comfort. Oral sedation is available for people who feel anxious.

Cost, insurance and planning both steps

A root canal and the crown that follows are separate procedures, so they are priced separately. What changes the cost of the crown includes the material, whether a core buildup or post is needed, and whether the tooth needs any gum or bite adjustments first. We confirm the exact price in writing before any treatment starts.

Insurance often treats the two steps differently. Many plans list root canals as basic services and crowns as major services, each with its own percentage, deductible and waiting period, and both count toward the same annual maximum. If your benefits are close to their limit, timing the crown can matter, so ask us to check before you schedule. Crowns after root canals: common questions

What happens if you don't get a crown after a root canal?

The tooth may be fine for a while, but a treated back tooth without a crown has a higher chance of cracking. If the crack reaches the root, the tooth often cannot be saved. Bacteria can also leak past a temporary filling and cause the infection to return.

How soon after a root canal should I get a crown?

Many dentists recommend placing the crown within a few weeks, and your dentist will set a timeline for your tooth. The temporary filling used after a root canal is not designed to last long. Until the crown is placed, chew on the other side and avoid hard foods.

Can I get a filling instead of a crown after a root canal?

Sometimes, especially on a front tooth that lost very little structure. Back teeth with thin walls or large old fillings usually need a crown to protect them. The decision depends on an exam of how much healthy tooth is left and how you bite.

Do front teeth need a crown after a root canal?

Not always. Front teeth carry less chewing force, so a well-sealed filling can work when most of the tooth is intact. A crown may still be suggested if the tooth is weak, already cracked or noticeably darker than its neighbors.

Does getting a crown after a root canal hurt?

Most people find it comfortable. The nerve inside the tooth has already been removed, so the tooth itself usually feels little, and the gums are numbed with local anesthetic. Some tenderness of the gums for a day or two afterward is common.
