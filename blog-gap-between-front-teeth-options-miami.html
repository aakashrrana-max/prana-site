How to close a gap between front teeth,
or decide to keep it.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

To close a gap between front teeth, dentists usually use one of three tools: tooth-colored bonding, porcelain veneers, or clear aligners that move the teeth together, and sometimes a combination. Which one fits depends on how wide the gap is, what caused it and how you want the rest of your smile to look. The cause matters most, because a gap that appeared recently can be a sign of gum disease. And keeping your gap is a perfectly good choice too.

Short answer: small gaps are often closed with bonding in a single visit. Wider gaps, or gaps with other spacing, often do better with aligners first, sometimes followed by bonding or veneers. Porcelain veneers start from $999 per tooth. If the gap is new or getting bigger as an adult, get your gums checked before any cosmetic work.

Why the gap is there

A gap between the two upper front teeth has a name, a diastema, and several possible causes. Figuring out which one applies to you shapes the plan, and it is the reason a good dentist asks how long the gap has been there.

  • Tooth size vs jaw size. If the teeth are slightly small for the arch, the extra space has to go somewhere. Small or narrow side incisors are a common reason for spacing around the front teeth.
  • A large frenum. The frenum is the band of tissue that joins the inside of the upper lip to the gum. When it is thick and attaches low, between the front teeth, it can hold them apart.
  • Habits. Long-term thumb sucking, or a tongue that pushes forward when you swallow, can push front teeth outward and open space.
  • Missing teeth. When a tooth is missing elsewhere, neighbors drift and spaces open up.
  • Gum disease. Bone loss around the front teeth can let them fan out and separate. This one needs attention before anything cosmetic.

In children, a gap between the top front teeth is common while adult teeth are coming in, and it often narrows as the canines erupt. That is usually a case for watching rather than treating.

A new gap in an adult: check your gums first

If you had no gap ten years ago and you have one now, or an existing gap is getting wider, don't start with bonding or veneers. Teeth that drift apart in adulthood can be a sign that the bone and gum holding them are breaking down. Other clues include bleeding when you brush, gums that look puffy or have pulled back, and teeth that feel slightly loose.

Closing that kind of gap cosmetically while the cause continues is a bit like painting over a leak. The first step is an exam with gum measurements and X-rays, then treatment of any gum disease. Advanced cases are referred to a periodontist (a gum specialist). Once the gums are stable, cosmetic options are back on the table.

The wide-tooth problem, and why aligners sometimes come first

Bonding and veneers close a gap by making the teeth wider. For a narrow gap that is barely noticeable, because each tooth gains only a sliver. For a wide gap, the two front teeth can end up looking square or oversized next to their neighbors, and the smile looks off even though the gap is gone.

There are a few ways around that. Clear aligners such as Invisalign can move the teeth so the space is shared out more evenly, or closed completely. Sometimes the side teeth get a little bonding or a porcelain veneer too, so widths stay in proportion across the smile. Complex orthodontic cases, such as gaps tied to a significant bite or jaw problem, may be referred to an orthodontist.

If a thick frenum is holding the teeth apart, closing the gap alone may not last. A minor procedure to release the frenum, done by a periodontist or an oral surgeon after a referral, is sometimes recommended, usually timed around orthodontic treatment.

Keeping the gap closed, or keeping the gap

Gaps like to come back. Teeth that were moved together tend to drift apart without a retainer, especially when a frenum or tongue habit is involved, so plan on wearing one at night for the long term. Bonding along the edges of a closed gap is checked and polished at your regular cleanings.

And you may decide to leave it. A gap between the front teeth is not a health problem when the gums are healthy and the bite works, and many people consider it part of their face. Just keep it clean: floss or an interdental brush between those two teeth, and regular checkups so a gap that starts to widen gets noticed early.

Gap teeth: common questions

Can a gap between front teeth be closed without braces?

Yes, small gaps are often closed with bonding, and veneers can close slightly larger ones. Both work by adding width to the teeth. For wide gaps, moving the teeth with aligners first usually gives a more natural shape.

Will the gap come back after it is closed?

It can, especially after orthodontic closure or when a thick frenum or tongue habit is involved. Wearing a retainer as directed is the main protection. Bonded edges are also checked at regular visits for chips or wear.

Is a gap between your front teeth bad?

Not by itself. A long-standing gap with healthy gums is a cosmetic feature, not a health problem. A new or widening gap in an adult is different and should be checked for gum disease.

How much does it cost to fix a gap in your teeth?

It depends on the method and how many teeth are involved. Porcelain veneers at Prana start from $999 per tooth, and bonding or aligner treatment is priced after an exam. The exact figure for your plan is put in writing before anything starts.

Can a child's gap between front teeth close on its own?

Often, yes. Gaps are common while adult teeth erupt and many narrow as the canines come in. A dentist can keep an eye on it and suggest an orthodontic check if it doesn't.
