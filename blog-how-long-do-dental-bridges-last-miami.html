How long does a dental bridge last?
As long as its anchors stay healthy.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

How long does a dental bridge last? Often many years, frequently more than a decade, but the honest answer is that a bridge lasts about as long as the natural teeth holding it up. The porcelain rarely wears out. What usually ends a bridge's life is decay or gum disease around an anchor tooth, often in a spot that is hard to clean. This guide explains why, how to clean under a bridge properly, and what to watch for.

Short answer: with good cleaning and regular checkups, many bridges last a decade or longer, and some far longer. The anchor teeth are the weak point, so cleaning under the false tooth every day with a floss threader, super floss or a water flosser matters more than anything else. A bad smell, swollen gums or a bridge that feels loose are reasons to call.

Why the anchor teeth decide everything

A traditional bridge is one solid piece: a false tooth (the pontic) joined to crowns on the teeth beside the gap (the anchors, or abutments). That design is strong, but it also means three teeth now share one fate. If one anchor runs into trouble, the whole bridge is affected.

Anchors are also asked to do more than they did before. They carry the chewing force for the missing tooth as well as their own. Longer bridges, bridges supported on one side only, and people who clench or grind put even more stress on them.

One problem deserves special attention. Sometimes the cement under one anchor crown washes out while the other end stays firmly attached. The bridge still feels solid, so nothing seems wrong, but saliva and bacteria seep under the loose crown and decay spreads quietly. By the time it hurts, the tooth can be badly damaged. This is one reason dentists check bridges with X-rays and test each end at checkups.

A few other things shorten a bridge's life: an anchor tooth that has had a root canal and is more brittle, gum recession that exposes the root under the crown edge, and frequent sugary sips through the day. Implant-supported bridges avoid decay on anchors entirely but have their own risk, inflammation around the implants if they are not kept clean.

Cleaning under the false tooth, step by step

Regular floss cannot pass between the pontic and the anchors, because they are joined. Plaque collects in that space and along the gum under the pontic. Here is how to clean it, once a day:

With a floss threader

  • Pull about a forearm's length of regular floss through the loop of the threader.
  • Push the stiff end of the threader under the bridge, from the cheek side toward the tongue side, close to the gum.
  • Pull the floss through until it is under the pontic.
  • Slide it gently back and forth under the false tooth, then hug the side of each anchor tooth and move up and down below the gumline.
  • Pull it out and repeat on the other side of the pontic if the bridge is longer.

With super floss

Super floss comes in pre-cut pieces with a stiff end, a spongy middle and regular floss at the other end. Thread the stiff end under the bridge, use the spongy part to wipe under the pontic, and the floss part for the sides of the anchors. Many people find it faster than a threader.

With a water flosser

Start on a low setting, aim the tip at the gumline, and trace slowly along the anchors and under the pontic from both the cheek and tongue sides. A water flosser is good at flushing out food and is easier for people with limited hand movement. It does not scrape sticky plaque as well as floss, so many dentists suggest using it along with a threader or small brushes rather than instead of them.

Small interdental brushes help where the spaces are wider, especially around implant-supported bridges. Finish by brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, angling the bristles toward the edges of the crowns.

If the whole bridge comes off, keep it in a clean container, avoid chewing on that side, and do not glue it back with household products. In some cases a dentist can clean and recement it if the anchors are healthy.

Habits that help a bridge last

  • Keep regular checkups and cleanings so the hygienist can clean under the pontic and the dentist can check each anchor. See our teeth cleaning page for what happens at those visits.
  • Wear a night guard if you grind or clench; bridges take sideways force badly.
  • Do not chew ice, hard candy or popcorn kernels on the bridge, and do not use it to open packages.
  • Keep sweet drinks and snacks to mealtimes, and drink water through the day, especially in the heat.
  • If your mouth often feels dry from medicines, mention it, since saliva protects the anchor teeth.

When a bridge needs replacing

A worn or failing bridge is usually cut off, because it is cemented as one piece. Once it is off, the anchors are examined closely. If they are healthy, a new bridge can be made much like the first one. If an anchor needs a filling, a buildup or a root canal, that is done first. If an anchor cannot be saved, the plan changes: a longer bridge, implants, or a removable partial may be the next step.

It is a good moment to rethink the gap. A bridge that made sense years ago might now be better replaced with an implant, especially if an anchor is failing. Our post on implant vs bridge cost compares the two.

Dental bridge lifespan: common questions

Can a dental bridge last 20 years?

Some do, especially when the anchor teeth and gums stay healthy and the area is cleaned well every day. Others need replacing sooner because of decay or gum problems around an anchor. Regular checkups help a bridge go the distance.

What is the easiest way to clean under a bridge?

Many people find super floss or a water flosser easiest, while a floss threader with regular floss works well and costs little. Pass floss under the false tooth once a day, or flush the area with a water flosser along with a threader or small brushes. Brush the crown edges at the gumline twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.

Why does my bridge smell?

A smell usually means food and plaque are trapped under the false tooth or around an anchor crown. Better cleaning often helps, but a smell that persists can be a sign of decay under the crown or a loose end, so it should be checked.

Can you get a cavity under a bridge?

The bridge itself cannot decay, but the anchor teeth can, especially at the edge of the crowns near the gum. Decay there can grow under the crown without pain, which is why X-rays and careful cleaning matter.

What happens if one side of my bridge comes loose?

Call soon even if it does not hurt. When one end loosens while the other holds, bacteria can get under the loose crown and cause decay that spreads quietly. If the anchor is still healthy, the bridge can sometimes be recemented.
