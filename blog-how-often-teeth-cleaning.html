How often should you get
your teeth cleaned?

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Most people should get a professional teeth cleaning every 6 months. If you have gum disease, smoke, are pregnant, or have certain health conditions, your dentist may recommend cleanings every 3–4 months instead. The only way to know your right interval is an exam.

How often should you get your teeth cleaned? For most people, the answer is every six months, but that's a general guideline, not a fixed rule — your gum health, habits, and history can move that number in either direction. Here's how to figure out what's actually right for you.

How often should you get your teeth cleaned?

For most healthy adults, a professional cleaning every six months is the standard recommendation, and it lines up with how most dental insurance plans are structured, typically covering two cleanings a year. That twice-yearly rhythm is enough for plaque and tartar to be cleared before they turn into bigger problems for most people, but it's a general starting point, not a universal rule.

Brushing and flossing at home slow down plaque buildup, but they can't fully replace a professional cleaning — a toothbrush simply can't reach everywhere a hygienist's instruments can, especially just below the gumline. Think of home care as daily maintenance and professional cleanings as the periodic deep check that catches what daily care alone misses.

Who needs teeth cleaning more often than every 6 months?

Some people benefit from a shorter interval, often every three to four months. This is typically recommended for people who:

  • Have gum disease (gingivitis or periodontitis) or a history of it
  • Smoke or use tobacco products
  • Are pregnant, since hormonal changes can increase gum sensitivity
  • Have diabetes or certain other conditions that affect gum health
  • Tend to build up tartar quickly regardless of home care
  • Have orthodontic appliances like braces that make plaque harder to remove at home

Can you go longer than 6 months between cleanings?

Some patients with consistently excellent home care, healthy gums, and low cavity risk may be fine stretching slightly longer between visits, but that's a decision a dentist should make based on your actual exam findings, not a guess. It's not something to decide for yourself, since gum disease in particular can progress without obvious symptoms until it's more advanced.

This is really the core point of the six-month guideline: it's a safe, well-studied default for the average patient, not a number that applies identically to everyone. Two people can look and feel the same day-to-day and still need very different cleaning schedules once a dentist actually examines their gums and checks for pocket depth around the teeth.

What actually happens at a teeth cleaning?

A typical cleaning includes removing plaque and tartar buildup (especially below the gumline, where a toothbrush can't reach), polishing the teeth, and usually a check for cavities and early gum disease as part of the same visit. Many practices combine the cleaning with an exam and X-rays as needed, so it's really a checkup and a cleaning in one appointment. See what to expect on our teeth cleaning page.

If tartar buildup or gum inflammation has progressed further, a dentist may recommend a more involved deep cleaning that reaches below the gumline in a way a routine cleaning doesn't. That's a different, more thorough procedure than a standard six-month cleaning, and it's only recommended when an exam shows it's actually needed.

What happens if you skip cleanings?

Plaque that isn't removed hardens into tartar, which a toothbrush can't remove on its own — only a professional cleaning can. Left long enough, that buildup is a major contributor to gum disease and cavities, and gum disease in particular can progress quietly before it causes pain or visible symptoms. Regular cleanings are one of the simplest ways to avoid bigger, more expensive treatment later, and pairing them with routine exams is how small issues get caught while they're still simple and inexpensive to fix. Questions, answered

How often should you get your teeth cleaned?

Most people should get a professional cleaning every 6 months. Some people with gum disease, certain health conditions, or a tendency to build up tartar quickly may need cleanings every 3–4 months instead.

Why does dental insurance usually cover 2 cleanings a year?

Most insurance plans are built around the standard 6-month recommendation for the average healthy adult, so they typically cover two cleanings a year. If you need more frequent cleanings for health reasons, ask your dentist and your insurance provider about coverage for additional visits.

Is it bad to go longer than 6 months without a cleaning?

For most people, yes — plaque hardens into tartar that only a professional cleaning can remove, and gum disease can progress without obvious symptoms. If it's been longer than 6 months, it's worth booking a visit rather than waiting for a problem to show up.

Does teeth cleaning hurt?

A routine cleaning is generally not painful, though it can feel like pressure or mild sensitivity, especially if it's been a while since your last visit or if your gums are inflamed. Tell your dentist if anything feels uncomfortable so they can adjust.

Can kids get their teeth cleaned too?

Yes — regular cleanings are recommended for kids as well, generally on the same twice-a-year schedule as adults, once a dentist has evaluated their specific needs. Prana is a family practice that sees kids, teens, and adults.
