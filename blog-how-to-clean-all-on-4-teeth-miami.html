How to clean All-on-4® teeth,
a daily routine that protects the implants.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

To clean All-on-4® teeth, you brush the bridge and gum line twice a day and clean underneath it every day with a water flosser, threaded floss or small interdental brushes. You cannot floss between the teeth the way you used to, because they are one connected piece. The goal is the same as with natural teeth: keep plaque off the gum where the implants come through, so the bone around them stays healthy.

Short answer: brush with a soft brush and a low-abrasion toothpaste, then clean under the bridge daily with a water flosser plus floss threaded under it or interdental brushes. Why fixed full-arch teeth need their own routine

A full-arch bridge is a single piece screwed onto four or more implants. On the side facing your cheeks and tongue it looks like teeth, but underneath there is a smooth surface that sits just above the gum. Food and plaque collect in that space and around the points where the implants meet the gum.

Implants cannot get cavities, but the gum and bone around them can become inflamed and infected, a condition called peri-implantitis. Daily cleaning under the bridge is what prevents it. Think of the underside as the part that matters most, even though nobody else ever sees it.

The daily routine, step by step

Most people settle into a routine of five to ten minutes at night and a shorter version in the morning:

  • Rinse after meals. Swish water to loosen food, especially after rice, shredded meats or anything with seeds.
  • Brush the teeth and gum line. Use a soft manual or electric brush, angled toward where the bridge meets the gum, on the outside and the tongue side. Spend extra time at the back ends of the bridge.
  • Use a single-tuft brush (a small pointed brush) around each spot where an implant comes through the gum, and at the very back of the bridge.
  • Water flosser under the bridge. Aim the tip along the gum line and under the bridge from both the cheek and tongue sides, moving slowly around the arch.
  • Thread floss under it. Floss with a stiff threader end and a spongy middle section can be passed under the bridge and wiped back and forth against each implant.
  • Interdental brushes fit into the gaps beside each implant. Your dentist can show you the right sizes, and coated wires are gentler on implant parts.
  • Finish with a low-abrasion toothpaste on the teeth. Ask whether an alcohol-free rinse makes sense for you.

A water flosser alone is not quite enough for most people, because it flushes loose food but does not wipe away sticky plaque. Using it together with floss or interdental brushes covers both.

What to avoid

  • Chewing ice, hard candy, bones, or opening packages with your teeth, which can chip or crack acrylic teeth
  • Very sticky foods while you are healing or if your dentist warns you about them
  • Abrasive toothpastes, whitening scrubs and charcoal powders, which dull and scratch the surface over time
  • Metal picks or toothpicks forced under the bridge, which can damage the gum
  • Smoking or vaping, which raises the risk of infection around implants
  • Grinding at night without protection; if you clench, a custom night guard may be recommended

Professional maintenance: when the bridge comes off

Home care keeps things under control, but some spots cannot be reached with the bridge in place. That is why full-arch patients have regular maintenance visits, often every three to six months at first, with the interval set by how your gums respond.

At these visits the dentist checks the gum around each implant, the bite and the screws, and cleans everything you can reach. From time to time the bridge is unscrewed and removed. The implants and the underside of the bridge are then cleaned and polished, the gum is checked closely, X-rays may be taken, and the bridge is screwed back in, usually in the same visit. Worn or chipped teeth can be repaired, and our in-house lab handles that at Prana.

Signs you should call

Call or text the office if you notice any of these, rather than waiting for the next scheduled visit:

  • Gums that keep bleeding after a week of careful cleaning, or look red and puffy
  • A bad taste, pus or persistent bad breath from under the bridge
  • The bridge feels loose, clicks or shifts when you bite
  • Pain or tenderness around one implant
  • A cracked or chipped tooth on the bridge
  • Gum pulling back so metal becomes visible

These can be early signs of infection or a loose screw, and both are easier to fix early. Our post on implant failure signs explains them in more detail. Swelling that spreads toward the eye or neck, fever or trouble swallowing needs the ER right away.

Cleaning All-on-4: common questions

Can I take my All-on-4 teeth out to clean them?

No. A fixed full-arch bridge is screwed onto the implants and only your dentist removes it, usually during maintenance visits. At home you clean it in place, including underneath with a water flosser and floss or brushes.

Is a water flosser enough for All-on-4?

For most people it is a big help but not enough on its own. It flushes food out, but sticky plaque on the implants usually needs the wiping action of threaded floss or interdental brushes. Using both gives better results.

How often should All-on-4 be professionally cleaned?

Many full-arch patients are seen every three to six months, especially in the first year. Your dentist sets the interval based on how your gums and home care look. The bridge is removed for a deeper cleaning periodically.

What toothpaste should I use with All-on-4?

A low-abrasion toothpaste is usually recommended, particularly for acrylic teeth. Gritty whitening pastes, charcoal and baking soda scrubs can scratch the surface and make it stain more easily. Ask your dentist which product suits your bridge.

Why do my gums bleed under my All-on-4 bridge?

Bleeding usually means plaque is irritating the gum around the implants. A week of careful cleaning under the bridge often improves it. If it does not, or you notice swelling, pus or looseness, call your dentist so the implants can be checked.
