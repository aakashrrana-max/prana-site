single-tooth implant vs. a 3-unit bridge: cost compared

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: A single implant often costs more upfront than a bridge, but because a bridge involves three crowned teeth and may need replacement sooner, the long-term cost gap is usually smaller than it first appears.

Cost is usually the first question once a patient decides they need to replace a missing tooth, and it's a reasonable one to ask before comparing options that can look very different on paper. Here's how a single-tooth implant and a traditional 3-unit bridge actually compare, beyond just the number on the initial estimate, so you're weighing the full picture rather than just the sticker price. A little context up front makes the conversation with your dentist much more productive too.

What Each Option Actually Involves

A single-tooth implant replaces one missing tooth with a titanium post and a crown, without touching or reshaping the teeth on either side of the gap. A 3-unit bridge replaces one missing tooth too, but it does so by crowning the two healthy teeth next to the gap and connecting all three crowns as a single piece, meaning three teeth are treated to fix a problem with one. That difference, one tooth treated versus three, is the starting point for any honest cost comparison, since it affects both the upfront price and what happens down the line. It also affects the amount of your own natural tooth structure involved: an implant leaves the neighboring teeth completely untouched, while a bridge permanently reshapes them.

Upfront Cost: Implant vs. Bridge

At Prana, a single implant with crown starts from $1,850, covering the one tooth that's missing, from the titanium post through the finished crown. A 3-unit bridge involves crowning three teeth rather than one, so pricing depends on the specific teeth involved, their condition, and what your exam shows, it's quoted individually rather than from a flat number the way a straightforward single crown might be. In general, treating three teeth costs more than treating one, which is worth weighing against what each option actually restores and how long it's expected to hold up.

The Cost a Bridge Can Add Later

A bridge relies on two healthy teeth to support it, and those teeth take on extra chewing force for as long as the bridge is in place, force they weren't originally designed to carry on their own. If either anchor tooth develops a cavity, cracks, or gum problems under the crown, treating it can mean redoing part or all of the bridge, which adds an unplanned cost years after the original work was done. An implant doesn't put that kind of ongoing load on neighboring teeth, which is one reason many patients see it as the more predictable long-term investment even when the upfront number is higher on day one.

What You're Getting for the Price, Not Just the Price

Comparing two numbers side by side only tells part of the story. At Prana, crowns for both implants and bridges are designed and milled in our in-house dental lab, so you're not paying a middleman markup for outsourced lab work either way. Implants also come with a 5-year workmanship guarantee, which is worth factoring into the comparison since it protects your investment if something related to the work itself needs attention down the line. When you're weighing cost, it's worth asking what's actually included with each option, not just what the headline number is.
