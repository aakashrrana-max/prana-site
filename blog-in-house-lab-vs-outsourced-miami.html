why we design and mill restorations in-house instead of outsourcing

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Outsourcing restorations to a separate lab adds shipping time, a middleman fee, and a technician who never sees your mouth. Prana keeps design and milling in-house, which is why it can offer same-day options, transparent pricing, and a 5-year workmanship guarantee.

Ask most dental offices who actually makes your crown, and the honest answer is: a technician at a separate lab company, working from a written prescription and a scan file, who has never met you or examined your mouth directly. That's the industry standard, and it's been the standard for decades. Prana does it differently, and the reasons come down to speed, cost, and accountability — three things that matter every time a restoration gets made.

How the traditional outsourced model works

In the conventional setup, a dentist takes a scan or impression, writes up a prescription describing the shape, shade, and material needed, and sends the file to an outside dental laboratory. A technician there — someone with no direct view of your mouth, bite, or gum tissue — designs and fabricates the restoration based on that written description alone. It then ships back to the dental office, typically taking one to two weeks, before it can be tried in and cemented. This model works, and it's how most crowns and veneers in the U.S. are still made, but it adds real time, cost, and a layer of guesswork between the exam and the finished product, since the person making the restoration never sees the patient it's for, and can only go by what's written down.

The costs of outsourcing that patients don't see

Every outside lab charges the dental practice a lab fee for each restoration, and that cost is built into what the practice then charges the patient — it's an extra business layer between your exam and your final crown, with its own overhead and profit margin sitting on top of what the dental office itself needs to charge. On top of that fee, there's shipping time in both directions, and if something doesn't fit quite right on try-in, the case has to go back to the same outside lab for a remake, adding another one to two weeks and another shipping cycle before it's resolved. None of that delay is anyone's fault exactly — it's just what the outsourced model involves structurally, regardless of how good the dentist or the lab technician are individually.

How the in-house model changes this

Prana designs and mills crowns, veneers, and full-arch restorations on-site instead of sending them to an outside company. There's no separate lab business taking a cut, no shipping delay, and no technician working blind from a written prescription — the dentist who scanned your mouth is the same person reviewing the digital design and the milled piece before it's placed. That's a large part of why many crowns can be completed the same day instead of over two visits with a temporary in between, and why adjustments, if needed, can happen immediately instead of requiring another shipping cycle to a facility you'll never see.

What it means for cost and accountability

Cutting out the outside lab and its markup is a meaningful reason Prana can publish upfront pricing like a $999 porcelain crown or a $999 porcelain veneer, rather than quoting case by case after adding an unpredictable lab bill on top. It's also why implant and full-arch restorations come with a 5-year workmanship guarantee — when one team designs, mills, and fits the restoration, there's no ambiguity about who stands behind the result if an adjustment is needed later, and no second company to point to when something needs revisiting.

Speed and consistency, not just cost

Beyond the financial side, having design and milling under one roof means the same standards apply to every case, since the same equipment and the same reviewing dentist are involved each time. There's no variation introduced by different outside labs handling different cases differently, and no waiting on a lab's own schedule or backlog to determine how quickly your restoration comes back. Consistency like that is hard to guarantee when a third party with its own workload is part of the process.

What to ask any dentist about their lab

If you're comparing providers, it's a fair question to ask directly: is this restoration made in-house, or sent to an outside lab, and how long will that add to my treatment? The answer affects your timeline, your cost, and who's accountable if the fit needs adjusting.
