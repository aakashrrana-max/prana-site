inside prana's in-house digital lab: why same-day restorations matter

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Prana designs and mills many crowns, veneers, and full-arch restorations in its own on-site lab instead of shipping cases to an outside facility. That usually means fewer appointments, faster turnaround, and tighter quality control from scan to final fit.

Most dental offices don't make your crown or veneer themselves. They take a scan or impression, send it to an outside lab, and wait days or weeks for it to come back before you can get your final restoration. At Prana, the lab is on-site — a room away from the treatment chair, not a facility across the country you'll never see. Here's what that actually changes for you as a patient, from your first scan to the finished piece.

What an in-house lab means in practice

An in-house lab means the same team that examines your teeth also designs and fabricates the restoration, using a milling machine and digital design software kept on the premises. Instead of an impression or scan traveling to a third-party lab technician you'll never meet, the file moves from the treatment room to the lab down the hall. There's no shipping step, no waiting on a courier, and no back-and-forth between two separate businesses trying to coordinate a fit. Everything from the initial exam to the finished crown or veneer happens under one roof, with one team accountable for the result. For patients, that mostly shows up as fewer appointments and fewer unknowns between the exam chair and the final restoration going in.

From digital scan to milled restoration

The process starts with a digital scan of your teeth rather than a mold. That scan becomes a 3D model on screen, which the dentist uses to design the restoration's exact shape, size, and bite alignment before anything is manufactured. Once the design is finalized, a milling machine carves the crown or veneer from a solid block of ceramic material in the lab on-site. Because the design and the milling happen in the same building, small adjustments — a contact point that's slightly too tight, a bite that needs fine-tuning — can be caught and corrected before the piece is ever placed in your mouth.

Why same-day matters for patients

Sending a case to an outside lab typically means wearing a temporary crown for one to two weeks while you wait for the permanent one to come back — and temporaries can loosen, chip, or feel different from your natural bite in the meantime. With the lab in the building, many crowns and other restorations can be designed, milled, and placed in a single visit. That means no second appointment just to swap out a temporary, no extra time off work for a follow-up visit, and no gap where a temporary restoration is doing a permanent job. For anyone juggling a busy schedule, cutting a two-visit process down to one is a real, practical difference, not just a nice-to-have.

Quality control from start to finish

When one team handles design and fabrication, there's no ambiguity about who's responsible for the fit. The dentist who examined your bite is the same person reviewing the digital design and checking the milled piece before it's cemented — not a technician working from a written prescription with no ability to see your mouth directly. If something needs adjusting, it happens immediately rather than requiring a remake shipped back and forth to a separate company, which is part of why Prana backs implant and full-arch work with a 5-year workmanship guarantee. That kind of accountability is hard to offer when two separate businesses are involved in making one restoration.

What this covers and what to expect

Prana's in-house lab handles crowns, veneers, and full-arch restorations. Every new patient visit starts with a free consult that includes an exam and a 3D scan, so you'll see exactly what's being designed before any milling begins, and can ask questions about the process directly rather than being handed a finished piece with no explanation. If you're weighing your options, current pricing is published upfront rather than quoted case by case after the fact, and it's worth bringing your questions about the lab process to that first visit.

Bilingual care built around the same workflow

The in-house lab process isn't a separate add-on service — it's simply how restorations are handled at every visit, for English- and Spanish-speaking patients alike. Whether you're coming in for a single crown or discussing a larger case, the explanation of how your restoration is designed and made is part of the normal conversation at your appointment, not something reserved for patients who ask specifically. If you're deciding between providers, ask each one directly whether your case would be designed and milled on-site or shipped elsewhere.
