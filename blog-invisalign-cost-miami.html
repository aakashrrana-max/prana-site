What actually changes the total price?

Invisalign pricing isn't one-size-fits-all because treatment length isn't either. The main factors are:

  • Case complexity: minor crowding or spacing needs fewer aligner trays than a more involved bite correction.
  • Treatment length: more movement generally means more months of treatment and more aligner trays.
  • Compliance and check-ins: how consistently aligners are worn affects whether treatment stays on schedule or needs adjustment.
  • Whether it's a full or partial treatment plan: some patients need to correct their whole bite, others just want to fix a few crowded or spaced teeth.

Invisalign vs. braces — which costs more?

For comparable cases, Invisalign and traditional metal braces are generally similar in overall cost. The bigger differences tend to be lifestyle ones: Invisalign aligners are removable for eating and brushing and are far less visible day to day, while braces are fixed to the teeth and can't be taken out. Neither option is inherently "better" — it depends on your case, your habits, and your preference.

Braces can sometimes handle more complex movements without relying on patient compliance, since they're fixed in place the whole time. Invisalign requires more discipline — trays generally need to be worn around 20–22 hours a day to stay on schedule — but many adults and teens prefer the flexibility of being able to take them out for meals, photos, or sports. Your dentist can tell you honestly which option fits your case and lifestyle better.
