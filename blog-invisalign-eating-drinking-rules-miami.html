Can you drink coffee with Invisalign in?
The food and drink rules, explained.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

Can you drink coffee with Invisalign in? The standard advice is no: aligners come out for everything except plain, cool water. That covers coffee and cafecito, tea, wine, juice, soda and even most sparkling drinks. The rule can feel strict at first, but there are good reasons for it, and with a few habits it fits into a normal Miami week of work coffees, dinners out and weekend brunch.

Short answer: take your aligners out to eat or drink anything other than plain water. Hot drinks can warp the plastic, colored drinks stain it, and anything with sugar or acid gets trapped against your teeth. Plan your meals and coffee into the time your trays are out, rinse or brush before putting them back, and keep the case with you.

Why only plain water

Aligners fit tightly over your teeth, which is how they move them. That tight fit is also why drinks behave differently with trays in. Liquid seeps under the plastic and sits there, instead of being rinsed away by saliva. Three problems follow:

  • Sugar and acid held against enamel. A sweet coffee, juice or soda pools in the tray for hours. That is a recipe for cavities and the chalky white spots that sometimes show up after orthodontic treatment.
  • Stains. Coffee, tea, red wine and dark sodas tint the plastic. A yellowed tray is much more visible than a clear one, which defeats the point.
  • Heat. Hot liquid can soften and warp the plastic. A tray that has changed shape no longer pushes teeth the way it was designed to.

Eating with trays in is a firm no: chewing can crack them, and food gets packed underneath.

The wear-time math

Most aligner plans ask for about 20 to 22 hours of wear a day. At 22 hours, that leaves roughly two hours for every meal, every snack, every non-water drink and cleaning. It disappears quickly when you sip:

  • Breakfast with coffee: 20 minutes
  • Mid-morning cafecito: 10 minutes, if you drink it at the counter rather than nursing it
  • Lunch: 30 minutes
  • Dinner: 45 minutes
  • Brushing and putting trays back in after each: about 15 minutes in total

That is already about two hours. A latte sipped across a whole meeting, or a long afternoon of snacking, eats the budget on its own. The practical fix is to bundle: drink your coffee with breakfast instead of an hour later, have dessert straight after dinner instead of later in the evening, and treat snacking as a meal. Eating out without stress

Restaurants, work lunches and family gatherings are where most people slip. A small routine makes them easy:

  • Keep a travel kit in your bag or car: the case, a folding toothbrush, a small toothpaste and floss picks.
  • Take trays out in the restroom or discreetly at the table, and put them straight into the case. Trays wrapped in a napkin are the classic way they end up in the trash.
  • Never slip them into a pocket. They can crack or pick up lint and germs.
  • After eating, brush if you can. If you can't, rinse your mouth well with water and rinse the trays, then brush when you get home.
  • At long dinners or parties, take the trays out once at the start and back in once at the end, rather than in and out with every drink.
  • Order your coffee with dessert so it fits in the same window.

Occasional long events happen. One evening of reduced wear is rarely a problem; a pattern of them is what slows treatment.

Keeping your aligners clean

  • Rinse trays with cool or lukewarm water every time you take them out, so saliva and plaque don't dry on.
  • Brush them gently, inside and out, with a soft toothbrush you use only for the trays and a little clear, unscented soap or the cleaner your dentist suggests.
  • Skip toothpaste on the trays. Most toothpaste is mildly abrasive and can scratch the plastic, making it cloudy.
  • Never use hot water or a dishwasher, and don't leave trays in a hot car.
  • Soak them only in products made for aligners, following the instructions.
  • Brush and floss your own teeth before putting the trays back in, so food isn't sealed under the plastic.

Invisalign food and drink questions

Can I drink coffee with Invisalign in?

It is not recommended. Hot coffee can warp the trays, coffee stains the plastic, and sugar or milk gets trapped against your teeth. Take the trays out, drink your coffee, then rinse or brush before putting them back.

Can I drink sparkling water with Invisalign?

Plain sparkling water is a gray area because it is mildly acidic, and flavored versions are more so. The safest habit is to take the trays out for it, or at least rinse with plain water afterward. Still water is the only drink that is clearly fine with trays in.

What happens if I drink with my aligners in by accident?

One accident is rarely a problem. Take the trays out, rinse them and your mouth with water, and brush when you can. If a tray looks warped after a hot drink, call your dentist before continuing to wear it.

Can I chew gum with Invisalign?

Not with the trays in. Gum sticks to the plastic and can pull it out of shape. Chew sugar-free gum only while the trays are out, and count that time against your wear hours.

Do I have to brush every time I eat with Invisalign?

Brushing before trays go back in is ideal, because food trapped under the plastic sits against your teeth for hours. When you can't brush, rinse your mouth and the trays well with water and brush at the next chance.
