can invisalign fix an overbite or crossbite?

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Invisalign can correct many mild to moderate overbites and crossbites, but the more severe or skeletal the issue, the less likely aligners alone will be enough. An exam is the only way to know which category applies to you.

Invisalign has a reputation for fixing crowding and gaps, but people often assume it can't touch bite problems like an overbite or crossbite. That's not quite accurate. Clear aligners can correct a real range of bite issues, though how far they can go depends on the specific case, and there's no way to know your own answer without an exam. Here's how to think about it before your consult.

What Overbite and Crossbite Actually Mean

An overbite describes upper front teeth that overlap the lower teeth more than they should when you bite down. A crossbite is when one or more upper teeth sit inside, rather than outside, the lower teeth when the jaws close, which can affect a single tooth or a whole section of the mouth. Both are common, and both can range from mild and mostly cosmetic to more significant, affecting chewing, speech, or even causing uneven wear on certain teeth over time. Because the range is so wide, whether Invisalign can address yours really comes down to the specific degree and cause behind it, not the label itself. Two people can both be told they have an overbite and be looking at very different treatment paths.

Where Aligners Tend to Work Well

For overbites and crossbites caused primarily by the position of the teeth themselves, rather than the underlying jaw bones, aligners are often a solid option. Invisalign can include features designed specifically to help guide bite correction as part of the aligner sequence, gradually shifting teeth into a more balanced relationship. Many patients with mild to moderate cases complete treatment with aligners alone and see a meaningful improvement in how their teeth meet. This is exactly the kind of case where a 3D scan matters, since it lets your provider see the true relationship between your upper and lower arches, not just how your smile looks from the front. Seeing that relationship clearly up front is also what allows a realistic number of aligner stages to be planned before you ever start.

When the Jaw Itself Is Involved

Some overbites and crossbites originate from how the upper and lower jawbones are sized or positioned relative to each other, not just where the teeth sit. Aligners move teeth through the gum and bone, but they don't reposition the jaw itself. In these cases, Invisalign alone may only partially improve the bite, and a more involved approach could be recommended. This isn't something you can determine by looking in a mirror. It takes an exam and imaging to see whether your bite issue is tooth-based, jaw-based, or a mix of both. Even in jaw-related cases, aligners sometimes still play a role as part of a broader plan, which is one more reason a proper evaluation matters more than a quick guess.

Why This Isn't a Do-It-Yourself Diagnosis

Mail-order aligner companies and generic online quizzes can't tell the difference between a tooth-based bite issue and a jaw-based one, because that distinction requires X-rays and a hands-on look at how your jaw closes. Treating the wrong type of bite problem with the wrong tool can waste time and money, or in some cases make chewing or jaw comfort worse rather than better. This is exactly why bite correction should start with a proper exam rather than a self-assessment, and it's worth being cautious of any service that offers to treat a bite issue without ever looking at your actual jaw in person.

Why Leaving a Bite Issue Alone Isn't Risk-Free

An overbite or crossbite that goes unaddressed doesn't necessarily get worse quickly, but it also doesn't tend to correct itself. Over years, uneven contact between upper and lower teeth can lead to uneven wear on specific teeth, and in some cases contributes to jaw strain or discomfort discussed elsewhere on this site under TMJ-related symptoms. None of this means every bite issue needs treatment right away. It does mean that "it's not bothering me" isn't quite the same question as "is this stable long term," and an exam is the only way to tell those two things apart.
