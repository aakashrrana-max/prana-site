do you still need a retainer after invisalign or braces?

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Yes. Whether you finish with braces or Invisalign, your teeth can drift back toward their original position without a retainer. Retention is a normal, ongoing part of keeping your results, not an optional add-on to skip once treatment ends.

Finishing your last aligner tray or getting your braces off feels like the finish line, but there's one more step that keeps that result in place: a retainer. It surprises some patients that this part of treatment isn't optional, and a few skip it once the excitement of "being done" wears off. Here's why it matters and what it actually involves, so it doesn't come as a surprise after you've already put in the work.

Why Teeth Move Back Without a Retainer

Teeth aren't cemented into fixed positions. They're held in the jawbone by ligaments that remain somewhat flexible for a period after active treatment ends, which is exactly what allowed Invisalign or braces to move them in the first place. That same flexibility means teeth can gradually shift back toward where they started if nothing is holding them in the new position. This isn't a sign that your treatment failed. It's just how teeth and bone behave, and it's the same for every patient regardless of which method straightened their teeth. Even people who never had orthodontic work see some natural shifting over a lifetime, which is part of why retention is treated as an ongoing habit rather than a short phase.

What Retainers Actually Do

A retainer holds your teeth in their new, corrected positions while the surrounding bone and ligaments stabilize around that new arrangement. Retainers come in a few forms, removable clear trays similar in appearance to aligners, and fixed wire retainers bonded behind the front teeth, and which one is recommended depends on your case and your provider's judgment. Either way, the retainer isn't doing the work of moving teeth anymore. It's simply preventing them from drifting once the active movement phase is done. Some patients are given a combination of both types, depending on which teeth are most prone to shifting back.

How Long Retention Actually Continues

Retention typically continues for longer than most patients expect, often starting with more consistent wear and gradually easing to fewer hours or nights per week over time, based on your provider's recommendation for your case. Many providers suggest some form of nighttime retainer wear indefinitely, since teeth can shift subtly over the years even long after treatment, independent of whether you ever had orthodontic work at all. Your specific schedule will be set at your visit rather than a generic timeline, since it depends on how your teeth responded to treatment. Sticking to that schedule, even once your teeth feel completely settled, is what actually keeps the investment you made in straightening them worthwhile over the years ahead.

What Happens If You Stop Wearing It

Skipping a retainer, even for a few weeks, can allow noticeable shifting to begin, especially soon after treatment ends when teeth are least stable in their new positions. If you've lost a retainer or it's been a while since you wore one, it's worth getting checked rather than waiting to see what happens. A misfitting retainer can also be uncomfortable to force back into place if teeth have already started to move, so catching it early matters. Trying to force an old retainer back into place on your own, without having your teeth checked first, isn't a great substitute for a proper visit.

If You've Lost or Outgrown Your Retainer

If your retainer no longer fits, cracked, or went missing a while ago, Prana Dental can take a look and discuss replacement options at a visit. It's also a reasonable time to have your bite checked more broadly. Book a visit and get it sorted before any shifting has a chance to set in.

A Few Habits That Make Retention Easier

Small habits go a long way here. Cleaning a removable retainer regularly, rather than only when it feels obviously grimy, keeps it comfortable and helps it last. Storing it in its case the moment it's out of your mouth, instead of wrapped in a napkin or set on a nightstand, is the single biggest thing that prevents it from getting lost or accidentally thrown away. And if a fixed wire retainer ever feels loose or you notice a wire poking, treat that as worth a call rather than something to wait out, since a detached wire retainer stops protecting your results the moment it comes loose. Building these into your regular routine, rather than treating them as extra chores, makes retention feel like a normal part of oral care rather than a burden left over from treatment.
