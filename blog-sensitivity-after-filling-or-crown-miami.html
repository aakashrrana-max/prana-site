Tooth sensitive after a filling or crown?
How to tell normal from not.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

A tooth sensitive after a filling or crown is common, and most of the time it settles on its own over days to a few weeks. The type of sensitivity tells you a lot: a quick zing from cold that fades in seconds is usually a tooth recovering, while pain that lingers, throbs or shows up by itself is a reason to call. This guide explains why treated teeth react, what helps, and the signs that need another look.

Short answer: brief sensitivity to cold or biting after a filling or crown is normal and usually fades within a few days to a few weeks. If the tooth hurts every time you bite, the filling or crown may be a little high and needs a quick adjustment. Pain that lingers after cold, throbs, or wakes you up can mean the nerve is inflamed and should be checked.

Why a treated tooth can feel sensitive

Inside every tooth is the pulp, a bundle of nerves and blood vessels. Treating a cavity or preparing a tooth for a crown is work done close to that pulp, and the pulp can react to it the way skin reacts after a scrape: a little irritated for a while, then calm again.

A few things make that more likely:

  • A deep cavity. The closer decay got to the nerve, the thinner the layer of tooth left to insulate it, and the longer it may take to settle.
  • Crown preparation. Shaping a tooth for a crown exposes a large surface of dentin, the layer under enamel that has tiny channels leading toward the nerve.
  • A temporary crown. Temporaries are held with lighter cement and do not seal as tightly, so cold sensitivity while you wear one is common and usually eases once the final crown is on.
  • Sore gum around the tooth. The gum can be tender for a few days from the band, clamp or cleaning around a crown edge, and it can feel like tooth pain.
  • A bite that is slightly high. More on this below, because it is the easiest problem to fix.

What you feel and what it usually means

Use this as a rough guide, not a diagnosis. If you are unsure, a quick call is better than guessing.

The pattern over time matters as much as the symptom. Sensitivity that is slowly fading is reassuring. Sensitivity that is getting worse, or changing from quick zings to a lingering ache, is not.

The high bite: a five-minute fix

When you are numb, you cannot fully feel how your teeth meet, so a filling or crown can end up a hair taller than it should be. You may not notice until the next day, when chewing on that side feels sore or the tooth seems to hit first.

A high spot does more than annoy you. Every bite bruises the ligament that holds the tooth, and that can keep a tooth sore for weeks or even irritate the nerve. It will not wear down on its own fast enough to help.

The fix is simple. The dentist has you bite on thin marking paper, finds the spot that touches first, and smooths it down, then polishes the surface. It usually takes a few minutes and normally does not need numbing. Many people feel the difference the same day.

What helps while it settles

  • Use a toothpaste made for sensitive teeth twice a day, and leave a thin film on the tooth at night rather than rinsing it away.
  • Brush with a soft brush and gentle pressure, especially along the gumline of a new crown.
  • Avoid very cold drinks, ice and very hot coffee for a while, or sip with the other side of your mouth.
  • Chew on the other side for the first few days, especially with a temporary crown.
  • Stop testing the tooth with ice water to see if it still hurts; repeated jolts keep it irritated.
  • An over-the-counter pain reliever can help if you have no medical reason to avoid it. Follow the label and ask your physician or pharmacist if you take other medicines.

If you grind or clench, the tooth may stay tender longer, because it is being loaded at night while it heals. Our post on teeth grinding at night explains the clues.

When sensitivity points to a bigger problem

Sometimes a tooth that had a deep cavity was already closer to trouble than anyone could tell, and the nerve does not recover after the filling. Dentists often describe this as the difference between an irritated nerve that will settle and an inflamed nerve that will not. Lingering cold pain, pain with heat, spontaneous throbbing and pain that disturbs sleep point toward the second. In that case a root canal may be the way to keep the tooth, and this is something a dentist confirms with tests and an X-ray, not something to assume.

It is also worth knowing that pain can travel. A tooth next to the one treated, or a tooth on the opposite jaw, can be the real source. That is one reason to call rather than conclude the new work is to blame.

Sensitivity after treatment: common questions

How long does sensitivity last after a filling?

For many people it fades within a few days, and after a deep filling it can take a few weeks. It should improve steadily. If it is getting worse, lasting longer than a few weeks without improving, or you feel pain when biting, call your dentist.

Is it normal for a new crown to be sensitive to cold?

Some cold sensitivity in the first days after a crown is cemented is common, and it is common with a temporary crown as well. It should ease over time. Cold pain that lingers or keeps getting worse is a reason to have the tooth checked.

Why does my tooth hurt when I bite after a filling?

The most common reason is that the filling is slightly high, so that tooth takes the force first. A quick adjustment usually fixes it. Less often, biting pain can come from a crack or from irritation of the nerve, which the dentist can check.

Can a filling cause a tooth to need a root canal?

The filling itself does not cause it, but a deep cavity may have already irritated the nerve before the filling was placed. Sometimes the nerve does not recover, and a root canal becomes the way to save the tooth. Lingering or spontaneous pain is the main warning sign.

Should I use sensitive toothpaste after a filling?

Many people find a toothpaste made for sensitive teeth helpful while a treated tooth settles. It works gradually, so use it twice a day for a couple of weeks. It will not fix a high bite or an inflamed nerve, so call if the pain does not improve.
