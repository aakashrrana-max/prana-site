teeth grinding at night: signs and solutions

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Nighttime teeth grinding, called bruxism, often shows up as morning jaw soreness, headaches, and visibly worn or flattened teeth, since most people have absolutely no memory of doing it at all while asleep. A custom night guard is the most common and effective way to protect your teeth from the ongoing damage grinding causes over time.

Most people who grind their teeth at night find out from someone else entirely, whether it's a partner overhearing the sound or a dentist noticing worn enamel during a routine checkup. It's considerably more common than most people tend to assume, and the resulting damage tends to add up quietly over years without much warning along the way, since the actual grinding happens while you're completely unconscious of it. Here's what to actually watch for, and what genuinely helps once it's confirmed.

The signs that point to nighttime grinding

Waking up with a sore or noticeably tight jaw, a dull headache near your temples, or teeth that feel unusually sensitive first thing in the morning are all fairly common signs worth paying real attention to over time. A partner hearing grinding sounds while you sleep is often the very first clue that something's happening, since the person actually doing the grinding is rarely aware it's even occurring at all while they're asleep. Jaw clicking or popping when you open wide can be another related clue worth mentioning at your exam. Some patients also notice indentations along the sides of their tongue, which can happen from unconsciously clenching during the day as well as at night. Increased tooth sensitivity to cold air or drinks, without any obvious cavity behind it, can be another subtle early clue worth mentioning at your next visit.

What grinding actually does to your teeth over time

Grinding applies far more force to your teeth than normal, everyday chewing ever does, and over months and years it steadily wears down the biting surfaces of your teeth, can cause them to flatten or chip along the edges, and sometimes leads to small hairline cracks forming that weren't there before. Existing fillings and crowns can also wear down or fracture considerably faster than expected if grinding continues unaddressed for long enough without any protection in place. In more severe cases, the jaw joint itself can become sore or fatigued from the repeated strain placed on it night after night, and some patients even develop popping or clicking sounds when opening and closing their mouth as a result.

Why it happens in the first place

Stress and anxiety are the most commonly recognized triggers behind nighttime grinding by far, but a misaligned bite, certain sleep disorders, caffeine, and alcohol consumed close to bedtime can all play a genuine contributing role as well in many patients. It's often a combination of several overlapping factors rather than one single, clear-cut cause, which is part of why identifying your own personal triggers can genuinely help alongside whatever dental treatment is recommended by Dr. Rana. Keeping a rough mental note of stressful stretches and whether symptoms flare up alongside them can be a useful thing to mention at your visit.

How a dentist confirms it's actually grinding

During an exam, Dr. Rana looks specifically for characteristic wear patterns on the biting surfaces of your teeth, jaw muscle tenderness when he checks, and other physical signs that point specifically toward grinding rather than some other, unrelated cause entirely. This is usually visible to a trained eye even if you've genuinely never noticed a single symptom yourself up until that point in time, which is exactly why regular exams catch things patients tend to miss on their own.

The most effective solution: a custom night guard

A night guard, custom-fitted specifically to your own teeth using an impression taken at your visit, creates a cushioned barrier that absorbs grinding forces and keeps them from wearing down your natural teeth or existing dental work over the months and years ahead. It doesn't actually stop the grinding itself from happening, but it protects your teeth from the real consequences, which is genuinely the part that causes lasting, expensive damage if left alone for too long. A custom-fitted guard also tends to be far more comfortable and durable than a generic one bought over the counter.

What to do next

If you recognize any of these signs in yourself or from something a partner has mentioned, it's genuinely worth having your teeth and jaw evaluated rather than waiting around for visible damage to eventually show up on its own down the road.
