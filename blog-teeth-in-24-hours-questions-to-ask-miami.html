Teeth in 24 hours,
what it really means and what to ask.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

Teeth in 24 hours usually means that dental implants are placed and a fixed set of teeth is attached the same day or the next day, so you don't go home without teeth. It is real and widely used, but the teeth you leave with are usually a healing set, and the final teeth come months later. Here is what to expect, who is a good candidate and the questions worth asking any office.

Short answer: with immediate loading, many people with enough healthy bone leave with fixed teeth within about a day of surgery. Those are usually temporary healing teeth; the final bridge (acrylic or zirconia) is made after the implants heal, commonly a few months later. Not everyone qualifies, and your scans decide.

What happens in the first 24 hours

On surgery day, any remaining teeth that can't be saved are removed, the implants are placed (often four to six per arch, as in All-on-4® and All-on-X), and the gums are closed. If the implants are stable enough when they go in, a fixed bridge can be attached to them right away or the next day. This is called immediate loading.

That bridge is screwed onto the implants, so it does not come out at night. It is usually made of acrylic and designed for the healing period: light enough to protect the implants and easy to adjust as the gums change shape.

What "permanent" means in the ads

Ads often say "permanent teeth in 24 hours". In most cases, "permanent" means fixed (not removable by you), not final. The implants are meant to stay for the long term, but the bridge you get on day one is usually replaced with a final bridge after healing.

Some offices make a final-quality bridge from the start. Others plan a separate final bridge in acrylic or zirconia. Neither approach is wrong, but you should know which one you are buying and whether the final teeth are included in the price.

Who is a good candidate

Immediate loading depends on the implants being firmly anchored on the day they are placed. That usually means:

  • Enough bone in the right places, confirmed on a 3D CBCT image
  • Gum disease and infections under control at the time of surgery
  • Health conditions such as diabetes well managed
  • Not smoking, or willing to stop around surgery and healing
  • Willing to follow a soft diet for the first weeks

If an implant isn't stable enough on the day, the safer choice can be to let it heal before attaching fixed teeth, with a removable denture in the meantime. A good office explains this possibility before surgery, not on the day.

10 questions to ask any office

  • Am I a candidate for fixed teeth on surgery day, and what could change that on the day?
  • Are the day-one teeth temporary or final? What material are they?
  • When are the final teeth made, and are they included in the price?
  • How many implants per arch are planned, and why?
  • Are extractions, grafting, imaging and sedation included?
  • Who places the implants, and who designs and makes the teeth?
  • What follow-up visits are included in the first year?
  • What happens if an implant doesn't heal?
  • Who do I call if a healing tooth breaks or something feels loose?
  • Is the full price confirmed in writing before treatment?

Teeth in 24 hours: common questions

Can my teeth really be fixed in one day?

For many people with enough healthy bone, yes: implants are placed and a fixed healing bridge is attached the same day or the next. The final teeth are usually made a few months later, after the implants heal. Your scans decide whether it is realistic for you.

How much do teeth in 24 hours cost?

It is priced as full-arch implant treatment. In Miami, that is commonly quoted around $20,000 to $30,000 per arch. At Prana Dental it starts from $9,999 per arch, with the exact price confirmed in writing before treatment.

Are teeth in 24 hours permanent?

The teeth are fixed, meaning you can't take them out, but the day-one bridge is usually a healing set. A final bridge in acrylic or zirconia is commonly fitted a few months later. Ask whether the final teeth are included.

Can everyone get teeth in a day?

No. It depends on bone quality, gum health, general health and whether the implants are stable when placed. Some people do better healing first with a removable denture, then getting fixed teeth.
