Why does my tooth hurt when I bite down?
Six common causes, and how to tell them apart.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

If your tooth hurts when you bite down, the most common causes are a crack in the tooth, a new filling or crown that sits too high, an infection at the tip of the root, a deep gum pocket, grinding or clenching, and, for upper back teeth, sinus pressure. Each one feels a little different, and each one has a different fix. This guide explains the clues you can notice yourself, the simple tests a dentist uses to find the real cause, and when biting pain needs to be seen the same day.

Short answer: sharp pain on one spot, especially when you let go of a bite, often points to a crack. Soreness that started right after new dental work suggests a high filling. A dull, throbbing ache with a tooth that feels taller suggests infection. Several sore upper back teeth after a cold may be your sinuses. Swelling, fever or a pimple on the gum means call a dentist promptly.

The six usual causes

Biting puts force on two things at once: the tooth itself and the thin ligament that holds its root in the bone. Pain on chewing means one of them is irritated. These are the causes dentists find most often:

  • A cracked tooth. A crack can be too small to see. When you bite, the pieces flex apart slightly and pinch the nerve inside, which gives a sharp, quick pain.
  • A high filling or crown. If new dental work sits a fraction of a millimeter too tall, that tooth takes the whole bite and its ligament gets bruised.
  • Infection at the root tip. When the nerve inside a tooth dies or becomes infected, inflammation builds at the end of the root. The tooth can feel raised and tender to any pressure.
  • A gum pocket or gum abscess. Food or bacteria trapped in a deep pocket beside one tooth can make the gum and ligament sore to bite on.
  • Grinding or clenching. Hours of force at night can leave several teeth aching in the morning, along with tired jaw muscles.
  • Sinus pressure. The roots of the upper back teeth sit just under the sinus floor, so a cold, allergy flare or sinus infection can make them ache when you chew.

Clues you can notice yourself

None of these clues is a diagnosis, but they help you describe the problem clearly when you call, and they hint at what is going on.

Two causes can overlap. A tooth that has been cracked for months, for example, can eventually lose its nerve and become infected, so the pattern may change over time.

How a dentist tells them apart

Finding the cause is mostly a matter of testing one tooth at a time and comparing it with its neighbors. At a toothache visit, Dr. Rana usually combines several of these:

Bite tests

You bite on a small plastic tool placed on one cusp at a time. Pain on a single cusp, particularly the moment you let go, is a classic sign of a crack. Thin marking paper shows whether a recent filling or crown is hitting before the other teeth.

Cold test, tapping and pressing

A cold stimulus on the tooth checks the nerve. A quick twinge that fades in a few seconds suggests a living nerve; pain that lingers suggests inflammation; no feeling at all can mean the nerve has died. Gentle tapping and pressing show whether the area around the root is inflamed.

Gum measurements

A probe measures the gum pocket around the tooth. A single narrow, deep pocket next to an otherwise healthy tooth can be a sign of a crack running down the root.

X-rays and 3D images

X-rays show decay, bone loss and a dark area at the root tip when infection is present. Cracks often don't show on X-rays, which is why the bite and cold tests matter. A digital 3D scan of the teeth can magnify worn spots and visible crack lines, and a CBCT, a 3D X-ray, is sometimes used when a root fracture or a hidden infection is suspected.

When biting pain needs a same-day visit

Call or text for a same-day appointment if biting pain comes with any of these: swelling of the gum or face, a pimple on the gum, a bad taste that keeps coming back, pain that wakes you up, or a piece of tooth breaking off. Those point to infection or a crack that is getting worse. Our guide to a cracked or chipped tooth explains which breaks can wait a few days.

Go to the ER instead if swelling spreads toward the eye or down the neck, or if you have a fever, trouble breathing or trouble swallowing. Before we open on Monday, November 16, 2026, see a dentist who is open now for anything urgent.

Mild soreness after a new filling isn't an emergency, but don't wait months either. A high spot is usually quick to fix, and leaving it can keep the ligament inflamed. Until you are seen, chew on the other side and skip ice, hard candy and crunchy nuts.

What fixes it depends on the cause

Treatment follows the diagnosis, so two people with the same pain can leave with very different plans:

  • High filling or crown: a small bite adjustment, often in a few minutes. Learn more about tooth-colored fillings.
  • Crack: often a crown to hold the tooth together. If the nerve is involved, a root canal may come first. A crack that runs deep into the root usually means the tooth can't be saved.
  • Root tip infection: a root canal to clean out the infection, or an extraction if the tooth can't be restored. Complex root canals may be referred to an endodontist.
  • Gum pocket: cleaning out the pocket, sometimes followed by a deep cleaning of the area.
  • Grinding: a custom night guard to spread the force while you sleep.
  • Sinus pressure: no dental treatment if the teeth test healthy; the sinus problem goes to your physician, and the teeth usually settle as it clears.

Biting pain: common questions

Why does my tooth hurt when I bite down but not otherwise?

Pain only on biting usually means the tooth flexes or the ligament around the root is irritated. A crack, a high filling or early inflammation at the root tip are common reasons. A dentist can sort them out with bite and cold tests.

Why does my tooth hurt when I release my bite?

Pain at the moment you open after biting is a classic sign of a cracked tooth. The pieces flex apart under pressure and snap back when you let go, which irritates the nerve. Unlike a bone, a cracked tooth doesn't knit back together, so it is worth having it checked before the crack grows.

Is it normal for a new filling to hurt when I bite?

Some sensitivity for a few days after a filling is common. Pain that is clearly on biting, or a tooth that hits first when you close, often means the filling is a little high. Call your dentist, because an adjustment is usually quick.

Can a sinus infection make my teeth hurt when I chew?

Yes. The upper back teeth sit right below the sinuses, so sinus pressure can make several of them ache when you chew or bend over. If the teeth test healthy, the pain usually fades as the sinus problem clears.

Does pain when biting mean I need a root canal?

Not necessarily. Many cases turn out to be a high filling, a crack that needs a crown, a gum problem or grinding. A root canal is usually needed only when the nerve is badly inflamed or infected, which the tests and X-rays help show.
