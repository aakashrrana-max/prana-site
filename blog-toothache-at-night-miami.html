toothache at night: what to do before you can see a dentist

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Toothaches often feel worse at night because lying down increases blood flow to your head and there are fewer distractions from the pain itself in the quiet. Rinsing with warm salt water, taking an over-the-counter pain reliever, and keeping your head elevated can help until you're seen, but facial swelling or fever means calling right away instead.

Toothaches have a well-known, frustrating habit of getting noticeably worse right around bedtime, which is genuinely exhausting when you can't actually see a dentist until the following morning and every hour feels considerably longer than it actually is. Here's why that actually happens physiologically, what genuinely helps get you through the night in the meantime without making things worse, and when to stop waiting altogether.

Why toothaches feel worse at night

Lying flat naturally increases blood flow to your head and face overall, which can increase the internal pressure inside an already inflamed or infected tooth that's already sensitive. There are also simply far fewer distractions available at night to take your mind off the pain compared to a busy daytime schedule, which genuinely makes it feel considerably more intense even when the underlying problem itself hasn't actually changed at all in severity from one hour to the next. A quiet, dark bedroom leaves little else to focus on besides the ache itself, which only adds to how bad it feels compared to how the same pain registered earlier in a busier, more distracting day.

Steps that can genuinely help overnight

Rinse your mouth thoroughly with warm salt water, which can help reduce inflammation somewhat and help clear away any debris that's irritating the area further. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever exactly as directed on the package label, and try sleeping with your head slightly elevated on an extra pillow or two to help reduce the blood flow pressure that genuinely makes lying completely flat feel noticeably worse overnight and into the morning. A cold compress held gently against the outside of your cheek can also take the edge off between doses, applied in short stretches rather than continuously against the skin.

What not to do, even though it's tempting

Don't place aspirin directly against the gum or tooth thinking it will somehow help relieve pain locally that way — it genuinely doesn't work like that at all and can actually burn the surrounding gum tissue instead of helping anything at all. Avoid very hot or cold foods and drinks entirely for now, and skip chewing on the affected side of your mouth completely until you're actually seen by a dentist who can help. Resist the urge to probe the painful area with your finger or a sharp object, which can introduce bacteria or make things worse. It's also worth resisting the temptation to double up on pain medication beyond what the label recommends, since that carries its own risks.

Signs that mean don't wait until morning

Facial swelling, a fever, difficulty swallowing, or pain that's rapidly getting noticeably worse rather than staying roughly steady overnight are all signs of a possible spreading infection that genuinely needs attention right away rather than waiting patiently for a regular scheduled appointment days later. Call Prana's emergency line immediately if any of these particular signs apply to your situation right now, day or night, rather than trying to push through until morning. Trust how quickly things are changing more than the pain scale alone, since a rapid shift over just a few short hours is usually the more important signal to act on right away rather than wait.

What a toothache is usually telling you

Persistent tooth pain, especially the specific kind that flares up mainly at night, is genuinely rarely something that simply resolves entirely on its own with time and patience alone — it's often decay reaching the nerve, a cracked tooth, or an active infection that genuinely needs a proper professional diagnosis to sort out completely. Getting through the night with home relief measures is perfectly fine as a stopgap, but it's not a real substitute for actually being seen by a dentist soon after the pain starts. Treating the discomfort at home buys you time, but it doesn't address whatever is actually causing it underneath the surface.
