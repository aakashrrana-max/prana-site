Use your dental benefits before year end,
what resets, what rolls over, what to book.

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated October 2026

If your plan runs on the calendar year, it pays to use your dental benefits before year end, because many plans reset the annual maximum on January 1 and unused benefits usually do not carry over. Deductibles start again too, and some flexible spending accounts have their own deadlines. Here is how to find out what you have left, what is worth doing now, and how to plan treatment that spans two plan years.

Short answer: check your plan year, remaining annual maximum, deductible and how many cleanings you have used. Unused maximum usually disappears when the plan resets, FSA money may be lost after your employer's deadline, and HSA money stays yours. Book early: December appointments fill fast.

What resets on January 1, and what does not

Many dental plans follow the calendar year, but not all. Some run on a plan year that starts on your employer's renewal date, so confirm yours before you plan around December 31. Here is how the main pieces usually behave at the reset:

A small number of plans have special rollover features that let some unused maximum carry forward if you had a checkup. Your plan documents or member services can tell you if yours does.

Five questions to ask your plan this month

Call the member number on your card, or log in to the plan's website, and write down the answers:

  • When does my benefit year start and end?
  • How much of my annual maximum is left? (Many plans have maximums often in the $1,000 to $2,000 range, so the remainder can be smaller than people expect.)
  • Have I met this year's deductible?
  • How many cleanings and exams have I used, and how far apart do they need to be?
  • Is there a waiting period on basic or major services, and has mine ended?

If that sounds like homework, send us your plan details instead. We check your benefits for you, and our guide to dentists that accept insurance explains the vocabulary.

What is usually worth booking before the reset

The most common year-end visits are simple: a checkup and professional cleaning you have not used yet, and fillings or other small repairs that were already recommended. Waiting on a small cavity rarely makes it cheaper; it can grow into a larger filling or a crown.

Bigger treatment needs more thought. One detail that surprises people: many plans count the date a crown or bridge is delivered, not the date the tooth was prepared. If a crown is started in mid-December but cemented in January, it may be paid from next year's maximum. Ask before you schedule so the timing works the way you expect.

Never delay something urgent, such as swelling, a broken tooth or pain that keeps you up, to fit a benefit calendar. Health comes first and the paperwork second.

Splitting treatment across two plan years

When you need several procedures, or one that happens in stages, spreading it across the reset can let you use two annual maximums instead of one. For example, gum treatment or a first group of fillings might happen in December, and the rest in January or February. Implant treatment, which naturally happens in stages over months, often falls across two benefit years on its own; our post on implants and insurance walks through that case.

A few cautions apply. The order and timing must make clinical sense first. If you are switching plans at open enrollment, a new plan may bring waiting periods or a missing-tooth clause, so finishing work under the old plan can sometimes be wiser. And if you are choosing FSA contributions for next year, a written treatment estimate helps you pick a realistic amount.

Year-end dental benefits: common questions

Do unused dental benefits roll over to next year?

Usually not. Most plans reset the annual maximum at the start of the new benefit year, and whatever you did not use is gone. A small number of plans offer a limited rollover feature, so check your plan documents.

When do dental insurance benefits reset?

Many plans reset on January 1, but some follow a plan year that starts on another date, often tied to the employer's renewal. Your member services line or online account will show your exact benefit period.

Can I use my FSA for dental work in December?

Usually yes, for eligible dental care, and that is often the reason December is busy. FSA money is generally use-it-or-lose-it, although some employers allow a short grace period or a limited carryover. Check your employer's rules and deadline.

Does my HSA expire at the end of the year?

No. Money in a health savings account rolls over from year to year and stays yours, even if you change jobs or plans. You can use it for eligible dental care at any time.

Can I split dental treatment between two years to use two maximums?

Often, if the treatment can safely be done in stages and both parts are covered by your plan. Many plans count the date a crown or bridge is delivered, so timing matters. Ask your dentist to plan the sequence with you.
