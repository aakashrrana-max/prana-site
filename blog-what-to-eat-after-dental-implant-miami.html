what to eat after getting a dental implant

By Dr. Aakash Rana, DDS · Updated September 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: Stick to soft, cool foods for the first few days, avoid anything hot, hard, crunchy, or sticky near the surgical site, and gradually return to normal eating as healing allows.

What you eat in the days after getting a dental implant can make a real difference in how comfortable your recovery is and how smoothly the site heals. It's not complicated, but a little planning ahead, having the right foods on hand before your appointment rather than figuring it out afterward, makes the first few days noticeably easier. Stocking your kitchen the day before your appointment, rather than after you get home feeling sore, is one of the simplest things you can do to set yourself up well.

The First 24 Hours: Soft, Cool, and Simple

Right after your procedure, stick to soft, cool, or room-temperature foods that need little to no chewing: yogurt, smoothies eaten with a spoon rather than sipped through a straw, mashed potatoes, applesauce, and blended soups that have cooled down all work well and won't disturb the surgical site. Avoid anything hot, since heat can increase blood flow to the area right when you want it to calm down and reduce swelling. This is also the time to take it easy generally, light activity, plenty of rest, and following any specific instructions from your visit rather than pushing through your normal routine. Keeping a water bottle nearby and sipping small amounts throughout the day, rather than gulping through a straw, also helps you stay comfortably hydrated.

Days 2-7: Soft But More Substantial

As initial soreness eases over the first couple of days, you can expand into things like scrambled eggs, oatmeal, well-cooked pasta, soft-cooked vegetables, and ground or shredded meats that don't require much chewing on the surgical side of your mouth. Staying hydrated matters throughout the whole recovery, and it's worth continuing to favor the opposite side of your mouth when you do chew anything firmer, even once you're feeling mostly back to normal and eager to return to your usual meals.

Nutrition That Supports Healing

Beyond just texture, what's in your food matters during recovery too. Protein-rich soft foods like eggs, yogurt, and blended soups support tissue repair, while staying well hydrated helps your body manage the natural inflammation that comes with any minor surgical procedure. Fruits and vegetables you can tolerate in smoothie or well-cooked form provide vitamins that also play a role in healing. None of this requires a special diet or supplements, it's simply worth leaning toward nutrient-dense choices over empty-calorie convenience foods while your body is doing extra work to recover.

What to Avoid Until You're Cleared

Skip anything hard, crunchy, or sticky near the implant site, think nuts, chips, raw vegetables, tough bread crusts, or chewy candy, since these can put unwanted pressure directly on the area while it's still healing underneath the surface. Very spicy or acidic foods can irritate healing tissue too, even if they don't cause pain elsewhere in your mouth. It's also worth avoiding alcohol and tobacco for the period recommended at your visit, since both can measurably slow healing and increase the risk of complications. If you're not sure whether a specific food you're craving falls into this category, it's always worth a quick call to ask rather than testing it out and hoping for the best.

When You Can Go Back to Normal

Most patients can gradually reintroduce a normal diet within a week or two as comfort allows, though the exact timeline depends on your healing and where in your mouth the implant was placed. If you're unsure whether a particular food is fine yet, it's always reasonable to ask rather than guess, especially in the days leading up to when your permanent crown, designed in Prana's in-house lab, gets attached and you start biting on it normally. Easing back in gradually, rather than jumping straight to your favorite crunchy snack, tends to be more comfortable even once you're technically cleared.

Signs Something's Off With Healing

Mild soreness while chewing anything in the early days is normal and expected as part of healing. Sharp pain, bleeding that won't stop, or swelling that gets worse instead of better after the first couple of days is not, and it's worth reaching out rather than waiting it out and hoping it improves on its own. Prana's emergency line is there for exactly this kind of situation, and you can also reach out through our office with any recovery questions along the way. Trust your own sense of what feels different from ordinary soreness, you know your body better than a general checklist ever could.
