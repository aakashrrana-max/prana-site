Replacing a tooth that was lost years ago

When a tooth has been gone a long time, a few things usually change. The bone where the root used to be tends to shrink. The neighboring teeth can tip into the space, and the tooth above or below may start to grow down or up toward the gap. None of that rules out an implant, but it shapes the plan.

Some people need a bone graft first to build up the area, and some need a neighboring tooth treated before an implant makes sense. The 3D scan at your first visit shows how much bone is left, which is where planning begins. If the case is complex, Dr. Rana refers you to an oral surgeon and keeps the rest of your care with us.

Longtime wearers of a partial denture often ask whether implants can help. For one or two missing teeth, an implant or an implant bridge can take the place of a removable partial. If most teeth are missing, a full-arch option such as All-on-4® may be worth discussing.

How the implant process works

Step one is a free first visit, made up of an exam, a 3D scan and a smile preview. If you need X-rays or a CBCT scan to plan the implant, we explain the reason and the cost before taking them.

A single implant is a post in the jaw, an abutment and a crown. After placement, the bone usually takes a few months to heal around it, depending on your bone and health, and then the crown from our in-house lab is fitted. Oral sedation is available for anyone who feels nervous. Each step is described more fully on our dental implants page.

Price, Medicare and Medicaid questions

The price for one implant tooth begins at $1,999 and takes in the implant, the abutment and the crown. If a graft or an extraction is needed, it's listed separately, and you receive the exact price in writing before treatment.

Older family members often ask about Medicare first. Under Original Medicare, implants are generally not covered. Some Medicare Advantage plans do include dental benefits, though they may pay for only part of the work. For adults on Florida Medicaid, dental coverage is limited and usually leaves implants out. We work with most major dental insurance as well, and we check your benefits for you.

Families who'd like to split the cost into monthly payments can apply for 0% financing options on approved credit through CareCredit, Cherry and LendingClub.

Little Havana implant questions

Can the whole implant consult be in Spanish?

Yes. The team is bilingual, so the exam, the explanation of your scan and the price discussion can all happen in Spanish. Family members are welcome to come along.

My father lost a tooth 20 years ago. Can he still get an implant?

Often, yes, but after that long the bone may have shrunk, and he might need a graft first. His health and medications matter as well. The 3D scan at his first visit shows where things stand.

Does Medicare pay for dental implants?

Usually not under Original Medicare, which generally leaves implants out. A Medicare Advantage plan with dental benefits may help with part of the cost. Bring the plan card and we check your benefits for you.

How do we get from Calle Ocho to the office?

Drive east on SW 8th Street toward downtown and then north on Biscayne Blvd, or take I-95 north to I-195 east and Biscayne. Either way it's usually 15 to 25 minutes, depending on traffic.

Can an implant replace my partial denture?

For one or two missing teeth, implants can often take the place of a removable partial. If several teeth are missing in different spots, the plan may combine options. Dr. Rana will compare them with you, including cost.
