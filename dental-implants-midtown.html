Younger professionals, growing families, one missing tooth

Midtown is mostly mid-rise and high-rise apartments around N Miami Ave and NE 2nd Ave, home to many younger professionals, couples and families with small kids. Tooth loss here often looks different from older neighborhoods: a front tooth knocked out playing sports, a molar that cracked after an old root canal, or a baby tooth that was never followed by an adult one.

Replacing that tooth while you're young has a practical side. Once a tooth is gone, the bone underneath can slowly shrink and the teeth beside it can drift into the space, so waiting years tends to make the eventual fix more involved.

Implants are for adults whose jaws have finished growing. If your teenager is missing a tooth, Dr. Rana can talk through short-term options until they're old enough, and kids and teens are seen here for regular checkups.

Single implant or implant bridge

A single implant replaces one tooth with its own post, abutment and crown, and leaves the healthy teeth next to it untouched. When two or three neighboring teeth are missing, an implant-supported bridge can fill the space on fewer posts. Which one fits depends on how many teeth are gone, where they are and how much bone the scan shows. For the full picture of how implants work, see our dental implants page.

If the tooth was lost to an injury, the scan also shows whether the bone around it was damaged, which affects the timing of the implant.

From first visit to finished tooth

Midtown implant questions

Can I walk from Midtown to Prana Dental?

Many people can. It's about a mile from the Midtown Miami shopping area to the office on Biscayne Blvd at NE 30th Street. If you're having oral sedation, line up a ride home instead.

Can I get an implant for a tooth I never had?

Often, yes. Some adults are born without certain permanent teeth and keep a baby tooth for years. When that baby tooth is lost, an implant is one common way to fill the space, provided there's enough bone. A 3D scan at your first visit shows whether the bone is there.

Is my teenager old enough for a dental implant?

Implants are usually placed only after the jaw has stopped growing, which is often in the late teens or later. Placing one too early can leave it out of line as the face keeps developing. Dr. Rana can check your teen and talk about temporary options until then.

How soon after losing a tooth should I get an implant?

Sooner is often simpler, because bone can shrink once a tooth is gone. Some implants are placed right after an extraction, while others need healing time or a graft first. It depends on the site, and the scan helps decide.

Do you see the whole family, or only implant patients?

The whole family. We see kids, teens and adults for checkups and general care, so a Midtown household can keep everyone at one office.
