## When to call 911 or go to the ER
Some problems need an emergency room, not a dental office. Call 911 or go to the ER if you have:
- **Swelling in your face or neck** with trouble breathing or swallowing.
- **Bleeding that won't stop** after 10 minutes of firm pressure.
- **A major injury to your face or jaw**, or you hit your head.

Not sure? If it feels life-threatening, call 911 first.

## Common dental emergencies
*If one of these sounds like you, call us. Problems like these often get worse when they wait.*
- **Severe toothache.** Pain that keeps you up or won't go away. We find out why it hurts. *Now: rinse with warm water. Don't put a pill on your gum.*
- **Broken or chipped tooth.** A crack, a chip or a piece that broke off. Some need care the same day. *Now: save the pieces. Chew on the other side.*
- **Lost crown or filling.** A crown came off or a filling fell out. The tooth may feel sharp or sensitive. *Now: keep the crown and bring it. Never use glue.*
- **Infection concerns.** A bump on the gum, a bad taste, or a swollen cheek. An infection won't go away on its own. *Swelling with trouble breathing? Go to the ER.*
- **Wisdom tooth pain.** Pain or swelling in the back of your mouth. We check if the tooth is stuck or infected. *Now: a cold pack on your cheek can help.*
- **Damaged fillings, crowns or dentures.** A broken filling, crown, bridge or denture. We check what can be fixed. *Now: bring all the pieces with you.*
- **A tooth that may need to come out.** We check first if the tooth can be saved. If it can't, we explain your options. If a specialist is a better fit, we will tell you.
