Your crown came off before a meeting: the next hour

A cracked tooth is a different problem from a lost crown. Sharp pain when you release a bite, or a twinge with cold that lingers, can point to a crack that may keep spreading, so stop chewing on that side and book a same-day visit rather than holding out until the weekend.

  • Find the crown and keep it in a small bag or container, not a napkin that ends up in the trash.
  • If the tooth zings when air hits it, skip cold drinks for now; the iced coffee can wait.
  • To get through the afternoon, a pharmacy dental cement or denture adhesive can hold some crowns in place temporarily. Never use super glue or household glue, which can damage both the tooth and the crown.
  • Chew on the other side and avoid anything sticky, like caramel or gum.
  • If it aches, an over-the-counter pain reliever taken as the label directs usually helps. Don't hold a pill against the gum, since that can burn the tissue.
  • Call or text (786) 579-0521 and tell us when your meeting ends. A temporary fix isn't a substitute for an exam.

When an open dentist or the ER is the right call

We open on Monday, November 16, 2026. If something happens before then, please see a dentist who is open now or go to an emergency room, depending on the problem. After opening day, some situations still belong in the ER rather than a dental chair: swelling that spreads toward the eye or neck, a fever with a toothache, any trouble breathing or swallowing, bleeding that won't slow with steady pressure, or a hard hit to the face. Those can signal a spreading infection or a fracture, and a hospital is equipped for both.
