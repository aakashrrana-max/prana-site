Who calls us from downtown

Downtown Miami mixes two crowds. On weekdays it is government buildings, courthouses and office towers, full of people whose hours are set by hearings, clients and commutes. On event nights it shifts to fans heading into Kaseya Center for a Heat game or a concert, and to festival-goers at Bayfront Park and Bayside Marketplace.

For office workers, the emergency is often a toothache that has built for days, or a filling that breaks over a desk lunch. For event-goers, it is usually an accident: an elbow in a packed crowd, a stumble on the stairs, a bottle that knocks into a front tooth.

Hurt at a concert or game? First steps on the way home

After a late show, keep any tooth or fragment moist and reach us when the office opens at 8am; for a knocked-out adult tooth, which is time-sensitive, a dentist who is open now or the ER may be the faster choice.

  • If a tooth is knocked out completely, pick it up by the white crown, not the root. Rinse it for a few seconds with milk or clean water if it is dirty, without scrubbing, then try to set it back in the socket and bite gently on a clean cloth, or carry it in a small cup of milk.
  • If a tooth is pushed out of place or feels loose but is still in, don't wiggle it to test it. Bite softly on clean gauze or a folded napkin and plan to be seen that same day.
  • For a split lip or cut gum, press a clean cloth firmly against the spot for several minutes. A cold can wrapped in a towel helps with swelling.
  • If you hit your head, feel dizzy or confused, or your bite suddenly feels wrong, skip the dental office and go to the ER.

A desk-day toothache

A toothache that throbs when you lie down, wakes you at night or comes with a bad taste can mean an infection or an irritated nerve, and it seldom settles down for good on its own. Rinse with warm water, floss gently in case something is stuck, and use an over-the-counter pain reliever only as the label directs. An early-morning visit before work is usually easier to fit in than a long lunch, since midday traffic near the courthouse can crawl.

Before November 16, and when to choose the ER

Until we open on Monday, November 16, 2026, an open dentist or an emergency room is where to go for anything urgent. Even after that, a hospital is the right first stop for a possibly broken jaw, heavy bleeding, a fever with facial swelling, or swelling that makes breathing or swallowing hard.

I hurt my tooth at a concert at Kaseya Center. Where should I go?

If you hit your head, can't stop the bleeding or your jaw feels wrong, go to the ER. For a chipped, loosened or knocked-out tooth without those signs, keep any tooth or piece moist and call or text (786) 579-0521. From November 16, 2026, same-day appointments are available Monday to Saturday, 8am to 8pm.

Can I get an emergency dental visit between meetings downtown?

That is what the 8am to 8pm hours are for, as long as you call or text first and tell us your window. The office is roughly 10 to 15 minutes north on Biscayne Blvd depending on traffic. Early mornings and evenings tend to be easier to reach than midday.

My tooth was knocked loose but didn't fall out. Is that an emergency?

Yes, it should be checked the same day. Don't wiggle it; bite gently on clean gauze and avoid chewing with it. A loosened tooth can often be repositioned and stabilized, and that tends to go better when it happens soon after the injury.

Can I take the Metromover to Prana Dental?

It gets you close, but not to the door. Metromover stops in the Omni area, about a mile and a half south of the office, so plan on a short rideshare, a Metrobus along Biscayne Blvd or a bike for the last stretch.
