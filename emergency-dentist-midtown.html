If your child hurts a tooth

Go to the ER if your child hit their head, seems drowsy or confused, can't stop bleeding, or has swelling that affects breathing or swallowing.

  • Stay calm, then check the mouth with a flashlight. Look for bleeding, a cracked tooth or a tooth that has shifted.
  • If a baby tooth is knocked out, don't try to put it back; that can harm the adult tooth developing underneath. Call or text so Dr. Rana can check the area.
  • If you aren't sure whether it's a baby tooth or an adult tooth, treat it as an adult tooth: hold it by the crown, rinse it briefly if it's dirty, keep it in milk and come in right away.
  • For a cut lip or tongue, press gently with clean gauze. A frozen fruit pop can ease swelling, and most kids accept it more readily than an ice pack.
  • Give children's pain medicine only at the dose on the package for your child's age and weight.

If the gym got you

Clenching hard under heavy lifts can contribute to cracks in back teeth, and a dropped weight or a stray elbow in a class can chip a front one. If biting suddenly hurts on one side, stop chewing there and avoid very cold drinks, because a cracked tooth can get worse under pressure. Save any piece that breaks off, rinse with warm water, and hold a cold pack on the cheek for swelling. If you spar or play contact sports, ask about a mouthguard at your follow-up.

Questions, answered

My child knocked out a baby tooth at the park. Is it an emergency?

It is worth a same-day call, but don't put a baby tooth back in. Press gauze on the spot to stop the bleeding, keep your child comfortable, and call or text (786) 579-0521 so Dr. Rana can check the gums and the adult tooth underneath. Go to the ER if there was a head injury or the bleeding won't stop.

I cracked a tooth lifting at the gym. What should I do?

Stop chewing on that side, avoid very hot and cold foods, and save any fragment. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever as the label directs if you need it, then call or text for a same-day visit. Cracks can deepen with use, so treating one early is usually simpler.
