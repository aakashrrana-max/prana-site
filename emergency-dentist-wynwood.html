Chipped a tooth on a night out: what to do

Facial trauma is a different story. If you fell hard, hit your head, blacked out even briefly, or your jaw doesn't close the way it used to, go to the ER first and deal with the tooth afterward.

  • Look for the broken piece. If you find it, keep it in a little milk or water; a fragment can sometimes be bonded back, and it tells Dr. Rana something useful either way.
  • Rinse your mouth gently with warm water. If your lip or gum is bleeding, press a clean cloth or napkin on it for about ten minutes.
  • Cover a sharp edge with a piece of sugar-free gum or pharmacy dental wax so it doesn't cut your tongue overnight.
  • Hold a cold pack wrapped in a cloth against your lip or cheek, on and off, to calm swelling.
  • Switch to water for the rest of the night and chew on the other side.
  • If the chip is small and doesn't hurt, it can usually wait for a regular appointment. If it hurts, is sensitive to air, or a large part of the tooth is gone, call or text for a same-day visit.

Questions, answered

I chipped a tooth in Wynwood on Saturday night. Can I be seen Sunday?

The office is closed on Sundays. Keep any broken piece in milk or water, cover sharp edges, and call or text (786) 579-0521 for a Monday same-day time. If you have heavy bleeding, swelling, a fever or a head injury, go to the ER or an open dentist instead of waiting.

Can a chipped front tooth be fixed the same day?

A small chip can often be smoothed or bonded at the same visit, but it depends on how deep the break goes. Dr. Rana checks whether the nerve is involved before recommending anything. You'll see the options and the cost in writing before treatment starts.

I work late shifts at a brewery. When can I come in?

From November 16, 2026, the office is open Monday to Saturday from 8am to 8pm, so the morning after a late close or the early evening before a shift can both work. Call or text with your schedule and we'll look for a same-day time that fits.

Is a chipped tooth an emergency if it doesn't hurt?

Not always. A small chip with no pain can usually wait for a regular visit, though it's worth covering any sharp edge. Pain, sensitivity to air or cold, a large missing piece, or a pink or bleeding spot in the break are reasons to be seen sooner.
