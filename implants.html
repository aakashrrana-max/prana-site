What a dental implant costs in Miami, and how to compare quotes

Implant quotes across Miami vary a lot, and the headline numbers are rarely measuring the same thing. One office may advertise a price for the titanium post alone, then add the abutment and crown later. Another may bundle everything, including the scan. Two quotes can look far apart on paper and end up close once you line them up item by item, or the reverse.

The fairest way to compare is to ask every office for an itemized quote and check it against the same list. Here is what to ask about:

  • Is the price for the implant only, or the implant, abutment and crown together?
  • What is the crown made of, and where is it made?
  • Is an extraction needed, and is it priced separately?
  • Is bone grafting expected, or only possible? What would it cost if it comes up?
  • Which X-rays or scans are needed, and are they part of the price?
  • Is sedation included, optional or extra?
  • Will you wear a temporary tooth while you heal, and is it in the quote?
  • Are follow-up visits during healing part of the price?

A low number that leaves out the crown is not a lower price. A clear, complete number is easier to plan around, even when it is not the smallest one you see. That is why our quotes list each part separately, with the exact price confirmed in writing before any treatment.

Missing several teeth, or a whole arch?

Replacing many teeth one implant at a time is usually not the most sensible route. When most of an arch is missing or failing, a fixed full-arch bridge on a few implants often makes more sense. Using dental insurance toward an implant

We accept most major dental insurance. Whether your plan pays toward an implant, and how much, depends entirely on how that plan is written, so we never promise coverage before we see your benefits. Here is the general picture in Florida:

  • Many dental plans have an annual maximum, often in the $1,000 to $2,000 range, so they may cover only part of implant treatment.
  • Some plans have waiting periods for major work, and some exclude implants altogether.
  • Original Medicare generally does not cover dental implants. Some Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits, which may help.
  • Florida Medicaid dental coverage for adults is limited and generally does not include implants.

Even when a plan excludes the implant itself, it sometimes contributes to related parts such as an exam, an extraction or the crown. You do not have to work any of this out alone: we check your benefits for you. For a longer explanation, read does insurance cover dental implants in Miami.

Are you a candidate for a dental implant?

Many adults who are missing a tooth can get an implant, but the answer depends on your bone and health. These are the things that usually matter most:

  • Enough jawbone. The implant needs solid bone around it. If bone has thinned after a tooth was lost, grafting can often build it back up.
  • Healthy gums. Active gum disease is usually treated first, because it can put an implant at risk.
  • General health. Conditions such as uncontrolled diabetes, and some medications that affect bone, may call for extra planning or a talk with your physician.
  • Smoking and vaping. Tobacco can slow healing and raise the chance of problems. Cutting down or quitting helps.
  • A finished jaw. Teens usually wait until the jaw has stopped growing.
  • Daily care. Implants need brushing, flossing and regular checkups, just like natural teeth.

How long the implant process usually takes

Most of the time is healing, not appointments. For many people the whole process takes a few months, and it can run longer when an extraction or grafting comes first. Here is the usual order:

1. Free first visit

An exam, a 3D scan and a digital smile preview. You learn whether an implant fits your situation, what else might be needed and a likely timeline. If your plan needs X-rays or a CBCT scan, we tell you before anything is taken.

2. Extraction or grafting, if needed

If a damaged tooth is still there, it comes out first. Sometimes the implant can go in at the same visit. Other times the area heals for a few months before the next step.

3. Placing the implant

The area is numbed, and oral sedation is available if you would like to feel more relaxed. Most people go back to normal routines within a day or two, with soft foods on that side for a while.

4. Healing

The bone grows around the implant and bonds to it. This usually takes a few months, and longer in some cases. You keep up normal life during this time, with short checkups.

5. Your crown

Once the implant is stable, we scan it and our in-house lab makes the crown to match your other teeth. It is fitted and your bite is checked.

Does insurance cover dental implants in Florida?

How long do dental implants last?

With good home care and regular checkups, the implant itself can last many years, and for many people decades. The crown on top takes the daily wear and may need replacing at some point. Smoking, gum disease and heavy grinding can shorten an implant's life, so a night guard and routine cleanings help protect it.

Is getting a dental implant painful?

The area is fully numbed, so most people feel pressure rather than sharp pain while the implant is placed. Afterward, some soreness and swelling for a few days is common, and many people manage it with over-the-counter pain relievers taken as directed. Oral sedation is available if you are nervous. Call us if pain gets worse after a few days or you notice fever or growing swelling.

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

Many adults with healthy gums and enough jawbone, or bone that can be built up with grafting, are candidates. Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and some medications that affect bone can raise the risk or call for extra planning. The exam and 3D scan at your free first visit are how we find out, and complex cases are referred to an oral surgeon.

How long does the implant process take?

For many people it takes a few months from placing the implant to fitting the final crown, because the bone needs time to bond with the implant. If a tooth has to come out first or bone needs grafting, healing time is added. The appointments themselves are fairly short. You get a likely timeline at your first visit, based on your scan.

Can a dental implant be placed the same day as an extraction?

Sometimes. When there is no active infection and the remaining bone is solid, the implant can often go in right after the tooth comes out. When the site is infected or the bone is thin, it usually heals first, sometimes with a graft, and the implant is placed a few months later. Your scan helps decide which path is safer for you.
