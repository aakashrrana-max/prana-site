Problems we fix

Does this sound like your tooth?

A crown is a custom cap that covers a damaged tooth. It makes the tooth strong again and looks like a real tooth.

Cracked or broken tooth

A crown holds the tooth together so it can't break further. Small chips may only need bonding.

A big old filling

When a filling gets too large, the tooth around it can crack. A crown or onlay protects it.

After a root canal

A tooth with a root canal gets brittle. A crown helps keep it from breaking.

Worn-down teeth

Grinding can flatten teeth over the years. Crowns can bring back their height and shape.

An old crown that looks bad

A dark line at the gum, a chip, or a loose crown. We can replace it with one that matches.

Pain when you bite

This can mean a crack you can't see. An exam tells us what's going on before we suggest anything.

Missing a tooth instead? That's a bridge or an implant. See dental bridges →

Explained simply

Crown or bridge? Here's the difference.

A crown covers a tooth you still have

  • For: a cracked, broken, worn or weak tooth
  • How: we shape the tooth, then fit a custom cap over it
  • Visits: often one or two
  • Price at Prana: from $999*

A bridge replaces a missing tooth

  • For: one missing tooth, or a few in a row
  • How: crowns on the teeth next to the gap hold a false tooth
  • Surgery: none
  • Other option: a single implant, from $1,999*

Not sure which one you need? That's what the free first visit is for. Dr. Rana will show you your options and tell you if something simpler would work.

Questions

Crowns, answered.

How much does a crown cost in Miami?

At Prana, zirconia and porcelain crowns start at $999 for patients paying without insurance. The price can be higher if your tooth needs a build-up, a root canal or X-rays first. Each of those is its own line, and you get the full price in writing before anything starts.

Can my broken tooth be saved?

Often, yes. It depends on how deep the break goes. A small chip may only need bonding. A bigger break usually needs a crown. If the nerve is hurt, you may need a root canal and then a crown. If the crack goes into the root, the tooth may need to come out, and then a bridge or implant can replace it.

If you have the broken piece, keep it in milk or water and bring it with you.

Should I get a crown, a bridge or an implant?

A crown fixes a tooth you still have. A bridge or an implant replaces a tooth that's gone. A bridge needs no surgery and is often done in a few weeks, but the teeth next to the gap get shaped. An implant leaves those teeth alone but needs surgery and a few months to heal. No option is right for everyone. Dr. Rana will walk you through what fits your mouth.

How long does it take?

Many crowns take one or two visits in total, because we make them in our own lab. If the tooth needs other care first, like a root canal, it takes longer. Dr. Rana gives you your timeline at your first visit.

Will my crown look natural?

We match the shape and color to your other teeth, and you see the design before it's made. We check the color with you before the crown is cemented. For front teeth, porcelain often gives the most natural look.

Does getting a crown hurt?

The tooth is numbed first, so most people feel pressure, not pain. Your gum may be a little sore for a day or two. If you're nervous, ask about sedation.

How long do crowns and bridges last?

Many crowns and bridges last a decade or more, and some last much longer. There is no fixed end date. It depends on the tooth underneath, your gums, whether you grind and how well you clean. Regular checkups help them last.

Does insurance cover a crown?

Many dental plans pay part of a crown. We accept most major dental insurance and check your benefits for you. Insured patients pay their plan's rate and their share, and you see that number in writing before we start.
