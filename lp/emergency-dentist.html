When to call 911 or go to the ER

Some problems need an emergency room, not a dental office. Call 911 or go to the ER if you have:

  • Swelling in your face or neck with trouble breathing or swallowing.
  • Bleeding that won't stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure.
  • A major injury to your face or jaw, or you hit your head.

Not sure? If it feels life-threatening, call 911 first.

What we help with

Common dental emergencies

If one of these sounds like you, call us. Problems like these often get worse when they wait.

Severe toothache

Pain that keeps you up or won't go away. We find out why it hurts.

Now: rinse with warm water. Don't put a pill on your gum.

Broken or chipped tooth

A crack, a chip or a piece that broke off. Some need care the same day.

Now: save the pieces. Chew on the other side.

Lost crown or filling

A crown came off or a filling fell out. The tooth may feel sharp or sensitive.

Now: keep the crown and bring it. Never use glue.

Infection concerns

A bump on the gum, a bad taste, or a swollen cheek. An infection won't go away on its own.

Swelling with trouble breathing? Go to the ER.

Wisdom tooth pain

Pain or swelling in the back of your mouth. We check if the tooth is stuck or infected.

Now: a cold pack on your cheek can help.

Damaged fillings, crowns or dentures

A broken filling, crown, bridge or denture. We check what can be fixed.

Now: bring all the pieces with you.

A tooth that may need to come out

We check first if the tooth can be saved. If it can't, we explain your options. If a specialist is a better fit, we will tell you.

Can I just walk in?

Please call or text first. We don't take walk-ins. Calling first lets us hold a time for you.

Should I go to the ER or a dentist?

For a toothache, a dentist is usually the right place. The ER can help with pain, but it usually can't fix the tooth. Go to the ER or call 911 if you have swelling with trouble breathing or swallowing, bleeding that won't stop, or a major injury to your face or jaw.

What can I do for the pain until I get there?

Rinse with warm water. Floss gently to clear anything stuck. Hold a cold pack on your cheek. A pain reliever from the store can help if it is safe for you. Follow the label. Don't put a pill on your gum.

Do you see kids?

Yes. We are a family practice. Kids and teens with a toothache or a hurt tooth are welcome.
