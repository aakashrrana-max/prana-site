Coming over from Midtown

Midtown sits close enough to Edgewater that many residents treat the trip as a short walk rather than a drive; by car it's a quick few minutes east toward Biscayne Blvd, depending on traffic and lights, ending at 3050 Biscayne Blvd, Suite 703, on the corner of NE 30th Street.

Parking nearby is available if you'd rather drive. Message us on WhatsApp as you head out and we'll have you checked in when you get here.

Weekend afternoons around the Shops at Midtown Miami bring in a lot of shoppers and diners, so a weekday visit usually means an easier walk or a quicker drive over.

Midtown's internal streets are laid out on a tighter grid than most of the surrounding area, built specifically around foot traffic between the shops and the residential blocks around them. That grid also makes the short hop over to Biscayne Blvd fairly direct, without much need to backtrack or double around a larger block.

Midtown's residents

Midtown Miami is a dense cluster of residential towers built around the Shops at Midtown Miami, on land that was once a Florida East Coast Railway rail yard — largely home to young professionals, couples, and renters drawn to how walkable the area is. Most errands here happen on foot within a few blocks.

If you've just moved into a Midtown rental and haven't found a dentist yet, no prior records are needed to get started — a baseline exam and a 3D scan is enough for a first visit.

Midtown's buildings are mostly newer, which means a lot of leases turn over every year or two — if you're new to Miami altogether rather than just new to the neighborhood, that's fine too; a first visit works the same either way, starting with our own exam and scan.

Built on old rail land

The Shops at Midtown Miami and the residential towers around them sit on what used to be a Florida East Coast Railway freight yard, redeveloped over the past two decades into one of the more walkable pockets of the city. That layout — wide sidewalks, buildings close to the street — is a big part of why a short walk covers most of what residents need.

Midtown also sits between two very different neighbors, Wynwood to the south and Edgewater's bayfront to the east, which means the area draws a mix of people heading in every direction rather than staying purely local to the shops themselves.

Compared to Wynwood's warehouse blocks or Edgewater's bayfront towers, Midtown reads as more purpose-built retail than either — the residential buildings sit around the shops rather than the other way around, which keeps a lot of daily foot traffic concentrated in a fairly compact area.
