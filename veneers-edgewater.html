Why living nearby helps with try-in and adjustment visits

Good veneers are rarely finished in one sitting. In many cases you wear a trial version first, either a mock-up or temporaries, so you can live with the shape for a few days. The final porcelain is tried in before it is bonded, and a short follow-up checks your bite and gumline.

Because the lab is in our own office, a note about an edge or a shade can often be acted on without shipping anything out. We're open Monday to Saturday, 8am to 8pm, so a neighbor can come by at lunch, after dinner or on a Saturday morning. A typical case looks like this:

  • Free first visit. An exam, a 3D scan and a digital smile preview of a proposed shape.
  • Planning. The X-rays and records Dr. Rana needs, then a written plan with your price.
  • Preparation and trial. Teeth are shaped where needed, and you leave in temporaries or a mock-up.
  • Try-in and bonding. Fit, length and shade are checked, then the porcelain is bonded.
  • Follow-up. A brief stop to polish and recheck the bite.

Who veneers suit, and who should wait

Veneers tend to work well for adults with healthy gums and sound teeth who want to change color, shape, small gaps or chips on the teeth that show when they smile. They aren't the right first step for everyone:

  • Cavities or gum disease get treated first, since porcelain bonded onto unhealthy teeth or gums may not hold up.
  • Heavy grinding can chip porcelain, so a night guard or another option may come into the conversation.
  • A tooth with a large filling may be better served by a crown.
  • If whitening alone would give you most of what you want, you'll hear that from us.

Prep, minimal prep, and how long veneers last

Most porcelain veneers need a thin layer of enamel removed from the front of the tooth so the result doesn't look bulky. That step is generally permanent, so we only suggest it when the benefit is clear. Some people, often those with small, worn or slightly set-back teeth, may be candidates for minimal-prep veneers that need little or no reshaping; the exam and scan tell us whether that applies to you.

With good habits, porcelain veneers can last many years, though the real number depends on your bite, any grinding, your gums and your care. Brush and floss as usual, keep regular checkups, and don't open packaging with your front teeth. Porcelain doesn't whiten, so whiten the surrounding teeth before the shade is chosen if you want them brighter.
