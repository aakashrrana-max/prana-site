How veneer prices in Miami compare

Veneer prices around Miami cover a wide spread, and one per-tooth number can describe very different things. Some offices quote porcelain while others quote composite resin under the same word. Some fold the trial smile and temporaries into the price, and others add them later. A quote that looks low can climb once planning records and extra visits appear, and a higher one may already contain them.

When you collect quotes, ask every office the same questions:

  • Is this price for porcelain or composite, and is it per tooth or per case?
  • How many teeth are in the plan, and why that number?
  • Will I see a preview or wear a trial version before anything permanent happens?
  • Will any enamel be removed, and roughly how much?
  • Who designs and makes the veneers, and where?
  • Which X-rays, cleanings or other treatment are needed first, and what do they cost?
  • What happens, and what would it cost, if a veneer chips years from now?

Put the answers side by side and the real gap between quotes is usually easy to see. Our written quote lists each of these items, so you can hold it up against anyone else's.

Porcelain vs composite veneers

Veneers come in two main materials. The choice changes the cost, the look up close and how long the result tends to hold up.

Composite is the same material used in dental bonding. For a single chipped edge or a small gap it can be a sensible, lower-cost answer, and it can usually be added to later. Porcelain tends to suit people changing several teeth at once, or anyone who wants a color that stays put for years. Our post on bonding vs veneers walks through more examples.

Prep vs minimal-prep veneers

Traditional porcelain veneers usually involve taking a fraction of a millimeter of enamel off the front of each tooth, so the porcelain sits flush and doesn't look thick. Enamel doesn't grow back. A tooth that has been prepared this way will generally need a veneer, or some other covering, for the rest of its life, and that is worth knowing before you agree to it.

Minimal-prep and no-prep veneers take off very little enamel, or none. They can work well for some people, often those whose teeth are small, worn down or sitting slightly behind the others, where a little added thickness looks natural. On teeth that are already full-sized or tipped forward, skipping preparation can leave a smile looking bulky. Whether you're a candidate depends on your tooth size, position and bite, and Dr. Rana will tell you plainly at your exam rather than steering you toward one style. More in who suits no-prep veneers.

How many veneers do you need?

The right number depends on how wide your smile is and what you want to change. One chipped or dark front tooth can be fixed with a single veneer, although matching one porcelain tooth to the natural teeth beside it takes careful shade work. Changing the color or shape of a whole smile usually means covering the upper teeth that show when you smile fully, which for many people falls somewhere between the front six and the front ten. Lower front teeth are added only when they show when you talk.

Your smile preview helps settle the question, because you can see what happens at the corners of your smile when fewer or more teeth are included. We suggest the smallest number that gets you the look you want. For common setups, see how many veneers a smile makeover needs.

When whitening, bonding, a crown or aligners fit better

Veneers are one option among several, and part of your first visit is ruling out what you don't need.

  • Color only. If you like the shape of your teeth and just want them lighter, professional whitening leaves the enamel alone and costs less.
  • Crowding or overlap. Invisalign can move teeth first. Some people find they no longer want veneers once their teeth are straight, and others need fewer of them or less preparation.
  • A weak or heavily filled tooth. A crown covers the whole tooth and protects what is left of it. Our post on veneers vs crowns compares the two.
  • A small chip. Bonding can often repair it in one visit without a lab step.

How long veneers last, and how to look after them

Well-made porcelain veneers often last a decade or longer, and some go well beyond that, but nobody can promise a number. How long yours last depends on your bite, whether you grind, how healthy your gums stay and the everyday habits you bring to them. Composite usually needs attention sooner.

Porcelain can't get a cavity, but the tooth underneath still can, so daily care is the same care you'd give natural teeth, with a little extra attention at the edges:

  • Brush morning and night with a soft brush and a gentle, non-abrasive paste, tilting the bristles toward the gumline.
  • Floss once a day, sliding the floss out to the side if it catches rather than snapping it up.
  • Keep your regular cleanings and exams, so small problems at the margins are found early.
  • Wear a night guard if you clench or grind.
  • Don't use your front teeth to open packages, bite nails or crunch ice.
  • If a veneer chips, feels loose or catches your tongue, call or text us rather than waiting for your next checkup.

Paying for veneers: financing and insurance

Financing

Most veneer cases cover several teeth, so many people choose to spread the cost. Zero-interest plans are available on approved credit through CareCredit, Cherry and LendingClub, and payments can start from about $167/month on approved credit. You'll see the monthly figure next to your written quote, and you can choose to treat every tooth at once or work in stages. Details are on our financing page.

Insurance

Dental plans usually treat veneers as cosmetic, so they are often not covered. There are exceptions worth checking. When a tooth is broken or decayed, part of repairing it may fall under a plan's restorative benefits, and sometimes that means a crown rather than a veneer. We take most major dental insurance. Ahead of time, we check your benefits for you, so the covered and uncovered parts are clear before you commit.

Questions, answered

How much do veneers cost in Miami?

Porcelain veneers at our Edgewater office start from $999 per tooth, so the main thing that sets your total is the number of teeth. Planning records, whitening of your other teeth, or treatment for cavities and gums are quoted separately, and only if you need them. The exact figure is written down for you before treatment starts, and 0% financing on approved credit can spread it out.

How long do veneers last?

Porcelain veneers often last a decade or longer with good care, though there is no fixed number. Grinding, gum health and habits like biting hard objects make the biggest difference. Composite veneers usually need repair or replacement sooner.

Do veneers ruin your teeth?

They don't ruin healthy teeth, though they do change them. Classic porcelain veneers usually involve shaving a little enamel from the front of each tooth, and that is permanent. The tooth underneath stays alive and can still decay, so brushing, flossing and checkups still matter. Minimal-prep options remove less enamel when your teeth suit them.

Can veneers fix crooked teeth?

Veneers can make mildly uneven or slightly crooked teeth look straighter, because they change the visible shape of each tooth. They don't move teeth or correct your bite. On more crowded teeth, straightening with clear aligners first often leads to a more conservative veneer plan.

Does insurance cover veneers?

Usually not, because most dental plans consider veneers cosmetic. If a tooth is broken or decayed, part of the repair may be covered under restorative benefits, sometimes as a crown instead of a veneer. Before you decide, we check your benefits for you.

Can veneers be whitened later?

No. Porcelain keeps the shade it was made in and doesn't respond to whitening gel. If you want your natural teeth lighter, whiten them before your veneers are made so the porcelain is matched to the brighter shade.
